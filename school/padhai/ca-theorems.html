📖 Saari Definitions (Units I–IV)

Section A me seedhe poochhi jaati hain — word-to-word yaad kariye.

Unit I definitions

📖 Definitions
  1. Analytic (holomorphic) function: \(f\) is analytic in an open set \(G\) if \(f'(z)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(z+h)-f(z)}{h}\) exists at every point of \(G\). Analytic at a point = analytic in some neighbourhood of it.
  2. Entire function: analytic in the whole plane \(\mathbb C\) (e.g. polynomials, \(e^z,\sin z\)).
  3. Region (domain): a non-empty open connected set. Simply connected: every closed curve in \(G\) is homotopic to a point (no holes).
  4. Harmonic function: \(u\in C^2\) with \(u_{xx}+u_{yy}=0\). \(v\) is a harmonic conjugate of \(u\) if \(u+iv\) is analytic.
  5. Branch of logarithm: a continuous \(g\) on \(G\) with \(e^{g(z)}=z\). Principal branch: \(\operatorname{Log}z=\ln|z|+i\operatorname{Arg}z\), \(-\pi\lt\operatorname{Arg}z\lt\pi\). Branch point: a point (here \(0,\infty\)) around which the function cannot be made single-valued; branch cut: the line removed (here \((-\infty,0]\)). Principal power: \(z^\alpha=e^{\alpha\operatorname{Log}z}\).
  6. Conformal map: preserves angles between curves in magnitude and sense. Isogonal: preserves magnitude but reverses sense (e.g. \(\bar z\)). Critical point: \(f'(z_0)=0\) (conformality fails).
  7. Extended complex plane: \(\hat{\mathbb C}=\mathbb C\cup\{\infty\}\) (Riemann sphere by stereographic projection).
  8. Möbius (bilinear) transformation: \(w=\frac{az+b}{cz+d}\), \(ad-bc\ne0\). Elementary maps: translation \(z+b\), rotation \(e^{i\theta}z\), dilation (magnification) \(rz\ (r>0)\), inversion \(1/z\).
  9. Fixed (invariant) point: \(T(z_0)=z_0\).
  10. Classification of Möbius maps: with two fixed points \(\alpha,\beta\): normal form \(\frac{w-\alpha}{w-\beta}=k\frac{z-\alpha}{z-\beta}\); elliptic if \(|k|=1\), hyperbolic if \(k>0\) real, loxodromic otherwise. With one fixed point \(\alpha\): parabolic, \(\frac1{w-\alpha}=\frac1{z-\alpha}+k\).
  11. Cross ratio: \((z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4)=\frac{(z_1-z_3)(z_2-z_4)}{(z_1-z_4)(z_2-z_3)}\) = image of \(z_1\) under the Möbius map sending \(z_2,z_3,z_4\) to \(1,0,\infty\).
  12. Symmetric (inverse) points w.r.t. circle \(|z-a|=R\): \(z^*=a+\frac{R^2}{\bar z-\bar a}\) (on the same ray from \(a\), \(|z-a||z^*-a|=R^2\)); w.r.t. a line: mirror images.
  13. Automorphism of a region \(G\): a one-one analytic map of \(G\) onto \(G\); they form a group \(\operatorname{Aut}(G)\).

Unit II definitions

📖 Definitions
  1. Zero of order \(m\): \(f(a)=f'(a)=\dots=f^{(m-1)}(a)=0\ne f^{(m)}(a)\); equivalently \(f=(z-a)^mg\), \(g(a)\ne0\). Simple zero: \(m=1\).
  2. Isolated zero: a zero with a neighbourhood containing no other zero.
  3. Maximum modulus on a circle: \(M(r)=\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|\); \(A(r)=\max_{|z|=r}\operatorname{Re}f(z)\).
  4. Local maximum of \(|f|\): \(|f(z)|\le|f(z_0)|\) for all \(z\) near \(z_0\).
  5. Convex function: \(\varphi(tx_1+(1-t)x_2)\le t\varphi(x_1)+(1-t)\varphi(x_2)\). (Three circles: \(\log M(r)\) convex in \(\log r\); three lines: \(\log M(x)\) convex in \(x\).)
  6. Unit disk \(D=\{|z|\lt1\}\); strip \(\{a\lt\operatorname{Re}z\lt b\}\); annulus \(\{R_1\lt|z-a|\lt R_2\}\).

Unit III definitions

📖 Definitions
  1. Singular point: a point where \(f\) is not analytic but every neighbourhood has points of analyticity. Isolated singularity: \(f\) analytic in \(0\lt|z-a|\lt R\). Non-isolated: e.g. \(z=0\) for \(1/\sin(1/z)\) (poles at \(1/n\pi\to0\)).
  2. Removable singularity: \(\lim_{z\to a}f(z)\) exists finitely (no negative powers in Laurent series).
  3. Pole of order \(m\): \(\lim(z-a)^mf(z)\) finite and non-zero (Laurent series has finitely many negative powers, lowest \((z-a)^{-m}\)). Simple pole: \(m=1\).
  4. Essential singularity: infinitely many negative powers in the Laurent series (neither removable nor pole).
  5. Principal part of \(f\) at \(a\): \(\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_{-n}(z-a)^{-n}\). Regular (analytic) part: \(\sum_{n\ge0}a_n(z-a)^n\).
  6. Residue: \(\operatorname{Res}(f,a)=a_{-1}=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint f\,dz\).
  7. Laurent series: \(\sum_{n=-\infty}^\infty a_n(z-a)^n\) valid in an annulus.
  8. Singularity at ∞: nature of \(f(1/z)\) at \(z=0\). Zero at ∞ of order \(m\): \(f(1/z)\) has a zero of order \(m\) at 0.
  9. Meromorphic function: analytic in \(G\) except for poles. Rational function: \(P/Q\), polynomials. Transcendental entire function: entire, not a polynomial (essential singularity at ∞).
  10. Winding number: \(n(\gamma;a)=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma\frac{dz}{z-a}\).
  11. Omitted (exceptional) value: a value \(w\) with \(f(z)\ne w\) for all \(z\) (e.g. 0 for \(e^z\)); Picard exceptional value.

Unit IV definitions

📖 Definitions
  1. Analytic continuation: \((g,G_2)\) is a direct continuation of \((f,G_1)\) if \(G_1\cap G_2\ne\emptyset\) and \(f=g\) on it.
  2. Symmetric region: \(z\in G\Rightarrow\bar z\in G\).
  3. Convergent infinite product: \(\prod(1+u_n)\) converges if at most finitely many factors vanish and the partial products of the rest tend to a non-zero limit. Absolutely convergent: \(\prod(1+|u_n|)\) converges (⇔ \(\sum|u_n|\lt\infty\)). If partial products \(\to0\) the product diverges to zero.
  4. Elementary (primary) factors: \(E_0(z)=1-z\), \(E_p(z)=(1-z)\exp(z+\frac{z^2}2+\dots+\frac{z^p}p)\).
  5. Order of entire \(f\): \(\lambda=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\frac{\log\log M(r)}{\log r}\); \(f\) has finite order \(\lambda\) iff \(|f(z)|\lt e^{|z|^{\lambda+\varepsilon}}\) for large \(|z|\), every \(\varepsilon>0\).
  6. Exponent of convergence of zeros \(\{a_n\}\): \(\rho_1=\inf\{\alpha:\sum|a_n|^{-\alpha}\lt\infty\}\). Rank \(p\): least integer with \(\sum|a_n|^{-(p+1)}\lt\infty\).
  7. Canonical product: \(\prod E_p(z/a_n)\) with \(p\) = rank. Genus: \(\mu=\max(p,\deg g)\) in \(f=z^me^{g}\prod E_p(z/a_n)\), \(g\) a polynomial.
  8. Principal part data (Mittag-Leffler): poles \(a_n\to\infty\) with prescribed principal parts \(P_n\big(\frac1{z-a_n}\big)\).

Applications of Schwarz lemma & maximum modulus

🔥 High
📜 Application 1Automorphisms of \(D\) are \(e^{i\theta}\frac{z-a}{1-\bar az}\) (T2.6); those fixing 0 are rotations.
📜 Application 2 (Schwarz for general disk)If \(f\) is analytic in \(|z|\lt R\), \(|f|\le M\), \(f(0)=0\), then \(|f(z)|\le\frac{M}{R}|z|\) and \(|f'(0)|\le\frac MR\).
✒️ ProofApply Schwarz lemma to \(g(z)=\frac1Mf(Rz)\), which maps \(D\) into \(\bar D\) with \(g(0)=0\): \(|g(z)|\le|z|\). Put \(z\to z/R\): \(\frac1M|f(z)|\le\frac{|z|}R\). Also \(g'(0)=\frac RMf'(0)\), \(|g'(0)|\le1\). \(\blacksquare\)
📜 Application 3If \(f:D\to D\) analytic has two distinct fixed points, then \(f(z)=z\).
✒️ ProofLet \(f(a)=a,f(b)=b\), \(a\ne b\). \(g=\varphi_a\circ f\circ\varphi_{-a}\) maps \(D\to D\), \(g(0)=0\), and fixes \(c=\varphi_a(b)\ne0\). So \(|g(c)|=|c|\) ⇒ \(g(z)=\lambda z\), and \(g(c)=c\) ⇒ \(\lambda=1\). Hence \(g=\) identity, \(f=\) identity. \(\blacksquare\)
📜 Application 4 (max modulus)(i) FTA (via minimum modulus): \(|p|\to\infty\), so \(|p|\) has a minimum on \(\mathbb C\) at some \(z_0\); if \(p(z_0)\ne0\), minimum modulus theorem forces \(p\) constant — so \(p(z_0)=0\).
(ii) A non-constant analytic function on a bounded region continuous up to the boundary with \(|f|=\)const on \(\partial G\) must have a zero in \(G\) (else min and max of \(|f|\) both on boundary ⇒ \(|f|\) constant).

Applications of Rouché and Argument principle

🔥🔥 Very high
📜 Application 1 (FTA by Rouché)A polynomial \(p(z)=a_nz^n+\dots+a_0\) (\(a_n\ne0\)) has exactly \(n\) zeros.
✒️ ProofLet \(f=a_nz^n\), \(g=a_{n-1}z^{n-1}+\dots+a_0\). For \(|z|=R\) large, \(|g|\le\sum|a_k|R^k\lt|a_n|R^n=|f|\). By Rouché \(p=f+g\) has as many zeros in \(|z|\lt R\) as \(a_nz^n\), i.e. \(n\). For larger \(R\) the count is the same, so all \(n\) zeros lie in \(|z|\lt R\). \(\blacksquare\)
📜 Application 2 (Hurwitz's theorem)If \(f_n\to f\) uniformly on compact subsets of \(G\), \(f\not\equiv0\), and \(\bar B(a;r)\subset G\) with \(f\ne0\) on \(|z-a|=r\), then for large \(n\), \(f_n\) and \(f\) have the same number of zeros in \(B(a;r)\). Corollary: limit of zero-free functions is zero-free or \(\equiv0\).
✒️ ProofLet \(\delta=\min_{|z-a|=r}|f|>0\). For large \(n\), \(|f_n-f|\lt\delta\le|f|\) on the circle. Rouché with \(f\) and \(g=f_n-f\): \(f+g=f_n\) has the same number of zeros as \(f\). \(\blacksquare\)
📜 Application 3Counting zeros in a disk/annulus (P4, P5); location of roots in a half plane via change of argument along the imaginary axis and a large semicircle.
📜 Application 4 (open mapping)A non-constant analytic function is an open map: near \(z_0\) with \(f(z_0)=w_0\) of multiplicity \(m\), every \(w\) near \(w_0\) is taken exactly \(m\) times (by the argument principle applied to \(f-w\)).

Applications of Mittag-Leffler, Weierstrass and reflection

🔥 High
📜 Application 1From \(\pi\cot\pi z=\frac1z+\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{2z}{z^2-n^2}\): \(\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac1{n^2}=\frac{\pi^2}6\).
✒️ ProofNear \(z=0\): \(\pi\cot\pi z=\frac1z-\frac{\pi^2}{3}z+O(z^3)\). Right side: \(\frac1z-2z\sum\frac1{n^2}\cdot\frac1{1-z^2/n^2}=\frac1z-2z\sum\frac1{n^2}+O(z^3)\). Compare coefficients of \(z\): \(\frac{\pi^2}3=2\sum\frac1{n^2}\). \(\blacksquare\)
📜 Application 2 (Wallis product)Put \(z=\frac12\) in \(\sin\pi z=\pi z\prod(1-\frac{z^2}{n^2})\): \(1=\frac\pi2\prod\frac{(2n-1)(2n+1)}{(2n)^2}\), so \(\displaystyle\frac\pi2=\prod_{n=1}^\infty\frac{(2n)^2}{(2n-1)(2n+1)}=\frac21\cdot\frac23\cdot\frac43\cdot\frac45\cdots\)
📜 Application 3Every meromorphic function on \(\mathbb C\) is a quotient of two entire functions.
✒️ ProofLet \(h\) be entire with zeros exactly at the poles of \(f\), with the same orders (exists by Weierstrass). Then \(g=fh\) has only removable singularities, so \(g\) is entire and \(f=g/h\). \(\blacksquare\)
📜 Application 4 (reflection)If \(f\) is entire and real on the real axis, then \(f(\bar z)=\overline{f(z)}\) for all \(z\) (identity theorem applied to \(f(z)-\overline{f(\bar z)}\), which vanishes on \(\mathbb R\)). Reflection also gives analytic continuation across a line/circle: across \(|z|=1\) by \(F(z)=1/\overline{f(1/\bar z)}\) when \(|f|=1\) on the arc.

Unit I — Analytic functions, Conformal and Möbius maps

T1.1 Analytic map with non-zero derivative is conformal

⭐ Medium
📖 DefinitionA map \(f\) is conformal at \(z_0\) if it preserves the angle between any two smooth curves through \(z_0\), both in magnitude and sense.
📜 TheoremIf \(f\) is analytic in a region \(G\), \(z_0\in G\) and \(f'(z_0)\ne0\), then \(f\) is conformal at \(z_0\).
✒️ Proof Let \(\gamma:[a,b]\to G\) be a smooth curve with \(\gamma(t_0)=z_0\), \(\gamma'(t_0)\ne0\). The image curve is \(\sigma=f\circ\gamma\). By the chain rule $$\sigma'(t_0)=f'(z_0)\,\gamma'(t_0)\ne0 .$$ Hence \(\arg\sigma'(t_0)=\arg f'(z_0)+\arg\gamma'(t_0)\) (mod \(2\pi\)). So the tangent of every curve through \(z_0\) is rotated by the same angle \(\arg f'(z_0)\).
If \(\gamma_1,\gamma_2\) are two curves through \(z_0\), the angle between their images is $$\arg\sigma_2'(t_0)-\arg\sigma_1'(t_0)=\arg\gamma_2'(t_0)-\arg\gamma_1'(t_0),$$ the same as the angle between \(\gamma_1,\gamma_2\), with the same sense. Hence \(f\) is conformal at \(z_0\). Also lengths are magnified by the factor \(|f'(z_0)|\). \(\blacksquare\)

T1.2 Existence of a branch of logarithm

⭐ Medium
📖 DefinitionIf \(G\) is open and \(g:G\to\mathbb C\) is continuous with \(e^{g(z)}=z\) for all \(z\in G\), then \(g\) is a branch of \(\log z\) on \(G\). The principal branch: \(\operatorname{Log} z=\ln|z|+i\operatorname{Arg}z\), \(-\pi\lt\operatorname{Arg}z\lt\pi\), on \(\mathbb C\setminus(-\infty,0]\).
📜 Theorem(a) A branch of \(\log z\) on \(G\) is analytic and \(g'(z)=1/z\).
(b) If \(G\) is simply connected and \(f\) is analytic and never zero on \(G\), then there is an analytic \(g\) on \(G\) with \(e^{g}=f\) (a branch of \(\log f\)).
✒️ Proof (a) \(g\) is one-one (since \(e^{g(z)}=z\)). For \(h\to0\), put \(k=g(z+h)-g(z)\to0\) (continuity) and \(k\ne0\). Then $$\frac{g(z+h)-g(z)}{h}=\frac{k}{e^{g(z)+k}-e^{g(z)}}\to\frac1{e^{g(z)}}=\frac1z .$$ (b) \(f'/f\) is analytic on \(G\). Since \(G\) is simply connected, \(f'/f\) has a primitive \(g_1\): \(g_1'=f'/f\). Then $$\frac{d}{dz}\big(f e^{-g_1}\big)=e^{-g_1}(f'-f g_1')=0,$$ so \(f e^{-g_1}=c\ne0\) is constant. Choose \(c_1\) with \(e^{c_1}=c\); then \(g=g_1+c_1\) satisfies \(e^{g}=f\). \(\blacksquare\)

T1.3 Möbius maps: group, decomposition, fixed points

🔥 High
📖 Definition\(S(z)=\dfrac{az+b}{cz+d}\), \(ad-bc\ne0\), is a Möbius (bilinear/linear fractional) transformation on \(\hat{\mathbb C}=\mathbb C\cup\{\infty\}\), with \(S(-d/c)=\infty\), \(S(\infty)=a/c\).
📜 Theorem(i) The Möbius maps form a group under composition.
(ii) Every Möbius map is a composition of translations, dilations/rotations and the inversion \(1/z\).
(iii) A Möbius map other than the identity has at most two fixed points in \(\hat{\mathbb C}\).
(iv) A Möbius map is uniquely determined by the images of three distinct points.
✒️ Proof (i) Associate \(S\) with the matrix \(\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\). A direct computation shows that \(S_1\circ S_2\) corresponds to the product of the matrices, whose determinant \((a_1d_1-b_1c_1)(a_2d_2-b_2c_2)\ne0\); so the composition is Möbius (closure). Composition of maps is associative. The identity \(z=\dfrac{1z+0}{0z+1}\) is Möbius. Solving \(w=\dfrac{az+b}{cz+d}\) for \(z\): \(z=S^{-1}(w)=\dfrac{dw-b}{-cw+a}\), with determinant \(ad-bc\ne0\). Hence a group.
(ii) If \(c=0\): \(S(z)=\frac ad z+\frac bd\) = dilation/rotation followed by translation. If \(c\ne0\): $$S(z)=\frac ac-\frac{ad-bc}{c^2}\cdot\frac{1}{z+d/c}.$$ So \(S=S_4\circ S_3\circ S_2\circ S_1\) with \(S_1=z+\frac dc\), \(S_2=\frac1z\), \(S_3=-\frac{ad-bc}{c^2}z\), \(S_4=z+\frac ac\).
(iii) \(S(z)=z\Rightarrow cz^2+(d-a)z-b=0\). If \(c\ne0\), this quadratic has at most 2 roots (and \(\infty\) is not fixed). If \(c=0\), \(\infty\) is fixed and the linear equation \((d-a)z=b\) has at most one root unless \(a=d,\ b=0\), i.e. \(S=\) identity.
(iv) If \(S,T\) agree at three distinct points, \(T^{-1}\circ S\) has three fixed points, so by (iii) \(T^{-1}S=I\), i.e. \(S=T\). \(\blacksquare\)

T1.4 Invariance of cross ratio

🔥🔥 Very high
📖 DefinitionFor distinct \(z_2,z_3,z_4\), the cross ratio \((z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4)\) is the image of \(z_1\) under the unique Möbius map \(T\) with \(T z_2=1,\ Tz_3=0,\ Tz_4=\infty\): $$(z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4)=\frac{(z_1-z_3)(z_2-z_4)}{(z_1-z_4)(z_2-z_3)} .$$
📜 TheoremIf \(z_2,z_3,z_4\) are distinct and \(S\) is any Möbius map, then \((Sz_1,Sz_2,Sz_3,Sz_4)=(z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4)\) for every \(z_1\).
✒️ Proof Let \(T z=(z,z_2,z_3,z_4)\); so \(Tz_2=1,\ Tz_3=0,\ Tz_4=\infty\). Put \(M=T\circ S^{-1}\), a Möbius map. Then $$M(Sz_2)=Tz_2=1,\quad M(Sz_3)=0,\quad M(Sz_4)=\infty .$$ The map \(z\mapsto(z,Sz_2,Sz_3,Sz_4)\) also sends \(Sz_2,Sz_3,Sz_4\) to \(1,0,\infty\). By uniqueness (T1.3 (iv)), \(M(z)=(z,Sz_2,Sz_3,Sz_4)\) for all \(z\). Put \(z=Sz_1\): $$(Sz_1,Sz_2,Sz_3,Sz_4)=M(Sz_1)=T(S^{-1}Sz_1)=Tz_1=(z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4).\ \blacksquare$$ Use: the Möbius map with \(z_k\mapsto w_k\) (k=1,2,3) is found from \((w,w_1,w_2,w_3)=(z,z_1,z_2,z_3)\).

T1.5 Cross ratio real ⇔ points on a circle; circles map to circles

🔥 High
📜 Theorem(a) Let \(z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4\) be distinct. Then \((z_1,z_2,z_3,z_4)\) is real iff the four points lie on a circle or a straight line.
(b) A Möbius map takes circles (lines counted as circles through \(\infty\)) onto circles.
✒️ Proof (a) Let \(S z=(z,z_2,z_3,z_4)=\dfrac{az+b}{cz+d}\). We show \(S^{-1}(\mathbb R)\) is a circle or line; it contains \(z_2,z_3,z_4\) (images \(1,0,\infty\)), so it is THE circle through \(z_2,z_3,z_4\), and \(z_1\) lies on it iff \(Sz_1\in\mathbb R\).
\(S z\) real \(\iff S z=\overline{S z}\iff \dfrac{az+b}{cz+d}=\dfrac{\bar a\bar z+\bar b}{\bar c\bar z+\bar d}\iff (a\bar c-\bar a c)|z|^2+(a\bar d-\bar b c)z+(b\bar c-\bar a d)\bar z+(b\bar d-\bar b d)=0.\)
If \(a\bar c\) is real the \(|z|^2\) term vanishes and this is a straight line \(\operatorname{Im}(\alpha z-\beta)=0\) with \(\alpha=a\bar d-\bar bc\ne0\). If \(a\bar c\) is not real, divide by \(a\bar c-\bar ac\) (purely imaginary): the equation becomes \(|z-\gamma|=\lambda\) with \(\gamma=\dfrac{\bar a d-b\bar c}{\bar a c-a\bar c}\), \(\lambda=\left|\dfrac{ad-bc}{c\bar a-\bar c a}\right|>0\) — a circle.
(b) Let \(\Gamma\) be a circle and \(z_2,z_3,z_4\in\Gamma\). For a Möbius \(S\), \(z\in\Gamma\iff(z,z_2,z_3,z_4)\in\mathbb R\iff(Sz,Sz_2,Sz_3,Sz_4)\in\mathbb R\) (T1.4) \(\iff Sz\) lies on the circle through \(Sz_2,Sz_3,Sz_4\). \(\blacksquare\)

T1.6 Symmetry principle and orientation principle

⭐ Medium
📖 Definition\(z,z^*\) are symmetric w.r.t. the circle \(\Gamma\) through \(z_2,z_3,z_4\) if \((z^*,z_2,z_3,z_4)=\overline{(z,z_2,z_3,z_4)}\). For \(\Gamma=\{|z-a|=R\}\): \(z^*=a+\dfrac{R^2}{\bar z-\bar a}\). For the real line: \(z^*=\bar z\).
📜 Theorem (Symmetry principle)If a Möbius map \(T\) takes \(\Gamma_1\) onto \(\Gamma_2\), then any pair of points symmetric w.r.t. \(\Gamma_1\) is mapped onto a pair symmetric w.r.t. \(\Gamma_2\).
✒️ ProofLet \(z_2,z_3,z_4\in\Gamma_1\); then \(Tz_2,Tz_3,Tz_4\in\Gamma_2\). Using T1.4 twice: $$(Tz^*,Tz_2,Tz_3,Tz_4)=(z^*,z_2,z_3,z_4)=\overline{(z,z_2,z_3,z_4)}=\overline{(Tz,Tz_2,Tz_3,Tz_4)} .$$ So \(Tz^*\) and \(Tz\) are symmetric w.r.t. \(\Gamma_2\). \(\blacksquare\)

T1.7 Möbius maps of upper half plane onto the unit disk

🔥 High
📜 TheoremEvery Möbius map of the upper half plane \(H=\{\operatorname{Im}z>0\}\) onto \(D=\{|w|\lt1\}\) is of the form $$w=e^{i\theta}\,\frac{z-\alpha}{z-\bar\alpha},\qquad \operatorname{Im}\alpha>0,\ \theta\in\mathbb R .$$
✒️ Proof Let \(T\) map \(H\) onto \(D\); then it maps the boundary \(\mathbb R\cup\{\infty\}\) onto \(|w|=1\). Let \(\alpha\in H\) be the point with \(T(\alpha)=0\). The points \(\alpha,\bar\alpha\) are symmetric in \(\mathbb R\); \(0,\infty\) are symmetric in \(|w|=1\). By the symmetry principle (T1.6), \(T(\bar\alpha)=\infty\). So $$T(z)=k\,\frac{z-\alpha}{z-\bar\alpha}.$$ For real \(x\), \(|x-\alpha|=|x-\bar\alpha|\) and \(|T(x)|=1\) give \(|k|=1\), i.e. \(k=e^{i\theta}\).
Conversely, the real axis is the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining \(\alpha\) and \(\bar\alpha\); a point \(z\) with \(\operatorname{Im}z>0\) lies on the same side as \(\alpha\), so \(|z-\alpha|\lt|z-\bar\alpha|\), i.e. \(|w|\lt1\). Hence such maps take \(H\) onto \(D\). \(\blacksquare\)
Special case: \(\alpha=i,\theta=0\): \(w=\dfrac{z-i}{z+i}\).

Unit II — Zeros, Modulus theorems, Schwarz lemma

T2.1 Zeros of an analytic function are isolated

⭐ Medium
📜 TheoremLet \(f\) be analytic in a region \(G\), not identically zero. If \(f(a)=0\), there is a unique integer \(m\ge1\) and an analytic \(g\) with \(g(a)\ne0\) such that \(f(z)=(z-a)^m g(z)\). Consequently the zeros of \(f\) are isolated (Identity theorem: if \(f=0\) on a set with a limit point in \(G\), then \(f\equiv0\)).
✒️ Proof Let \(A=\{z\in G: f^{(n)}(z)=0\ \forall n\ge0\}\). \(A\) is closed (each \(f^{(n)}\) is continuous). \(A\) is open: if \(z_0\in A\), the Taylor series of \(f\) about \(z_0\) is identically 0, so \(f\equiv0\) on a disk about \(z_0\), which lies in \(A\). As \(G\) is connected, \(A=\emptyset\) or \(A=G\). Since \(f\not\equiv0\), \(A=\emptyset\).
So at a zero \(a\) there is a least \(m\ge1\) with \(f^{(m)}(a)\ne0\). In a disk \(|z-a|\lt R\): $$f(z)=\sum_{n\ge m}a_n(z-a)^n=(z-a)^m\sum_{k\ge0}a_{m+k}(z-a)^k=(z-a)^mg(z),$$ \(g(a)=a_m=f^{(m)}(a)/m!\ne0\). Define \(g=f/(z-a)^m\) outside the disk; \(g\) is analytic on \(G\). By continuity \(g\ne0\) on some disk \(|z-a|\lt\delta\), so \(f(z)\ne0\) for \(0\lt|z-a|\lt\delta\): the zero \(a\) is isolated. \(\blacksquare\)

T2.2 Maximum modulus theorem

🔥🔥 Very high
📜 Theorem(First form) If \(f\) is analytic in a region \(G\) and \(a\in G\) with \(|f(a)|\ge|f(z)|\) for all \(z\in G\), then \(f\) is constant.
(Second form) If \(G\) is bounded, \(f\) is continuous on \(\bar G\) and analytic in \(G\), then \(\max\{|f(z)|:z\in\bar G\}\) is attained on \(\partial G\).
✒️ Proof First form. Let \(M=|f(a)|\). Choose \(\bar B(a;r)\subset G\). By Cauchy's integral formula (Gauss mean value theorem) $$f(a)=\frac1{2\pi}\int_0^{2\pi}f(a+re^{it})\,dt .$$ Hence \(M=|f(a)|\le\dfrac1{2\pi}\displaystyle\int_0^{2\pi}|f(a+re^{it})|\,dt\le M\), so $$\int_0^{2\pi}\big(M-|f(a+re^{it})|\big)dt=0 .$$ The integrand is continuous and \(\ge0\), so it is identically 0: \(|f|=M\) on every circle \(|z-a|=r\) (for all small \(r\)), i.e. \(|f|\) is constant on a disk \(B(a;R)\).
If \(|f|=M\) is constant on a disk, \(f\) is constant there: if \(M=0\) clear; otherwise \(u^2+v^2=M^2\) gives \(uu_x+vv_x=0,\ uu_y+vv_y=0\); with Cauchy–Riemann \(u_x=v_y,\ u_y=-v_x\) we get \(uu_x-vu_y=0,\ uu_y+vu_x=0\) ⇒ \((u^2+v^2)u_x=0=(u^2+v^2)u_y\) ⇒ \(u_x=u_y=0\), similarly \(v_x=v_y=0\). So \(f\) is constant on \(B(a;R)\).
By the identity theorem (T2.1) applied to \(f-f(a)\), \(f\) is constant on the whole region \(G\).
Second form. \(\bar G\) is compact, so \(|f|\) attains its max at some \(a\in\bar G\). If \(a\in G\), by the first form \(f\) is constant on the component of \(G\) containing \(a\), and then by continuity the same value is attained on the boundary of that component, which is in \(\partial G\). So the max is attained on \(\partial G\). \(\blacksquare\)

T2.3 Minimum modulus theorem

🔥 High
📜 TheoremIf \(f\) is analytic and non-constant in a region \(G\) and \(f(z)\ne0\) in \(G\), then \(|f|\) cannot attain its minimum in \(G\). (If \(G\) bounded, \(f\) continuous on \(\bar G\), never zero, the minimum of \(|f|\) on \(\bar G\) is attained on \(\partial G\).)
✒️ ProofSince \(f\ne0\) in \(G\), \(g=1/f\) is analytic in \(G\) and non-constant. If \(|f|\) had a minimum at \(a\in G\), i.e. \(|f(a)|\le|f(z)|\), then \(|g(a)|\ge|g(z)|\) for all \(z\in G\); by the maximum modulus theorem \(g\), hence \(f\), is constant — contradiction. \(\blacksquare\)
Note: the condition \(f\ne0\) is needed: \(f(z)=z\) on \(|z|\lt1\) has min \(|f(0)|=0\) inside.

T2.4 Schwarz lemma

🔥🔥 Very high
📜 TheoremLet \(D=\{z:|z|\lt1\}\) and let \(f\) be analytic in \(D\) with (a) \(|f(z)|\le1\) for \(z\in D\), (b) \(f(0)=0\). Then \(|f'(0)|\le1\) and \(|f(z)|\le|z|\) for all \(z\in D\). Moreover, if \(|f'(0)|=1\) or \(|f(z)|=|z|\) for some \(z\ne0\), then there is a constant \(c\), \(|c|=1\), with \(f(z)=cz\) for all \(z\in D\).
✒️ Proof Define \(g(z)=\dfrac{f(z)}{z}\) for \(z\ne0\) and \(g(0)=f'(0)\). Since \(f(0)=0\), \(f(z)=\sum_{n\ge1}a_nz^n\) and \(g(z)=\sum_{n\ge1}a_nz^{n-1}\), so \(g\) is analytic in \(D\).
Fix \(0\lt r\lt1\). On \(|z|=r\): \(|g(z)|=\dfrac{|f(z)|}{r}\le\dfrac1r\). By the maximum modulus theorem, \(|g(z)|\le\dfrac1r\) for \(|z|\le r\). Let \(r\to1^-\): \(|g(z)|\le1\) for all \(z\in D\). Hence $$|f(z)|\le|z|\quad\text{and}\quad|f'(0)|=|g(0)|\le1 .$$ If \(|f(z_0)|=|z_0|\) for some \(z_0\ne0\), or \(|f'(0)|=1\), then \(|g|\) attains its maximum value 1 at an interior point of \(D\). By the maximum modulus theorem \(g\equiv c\) with \(|c|=1\), so \(f(z)=cz\). \(\blacksquare\)

T2.5 The maps \(\varphi_a\) and Schwarz–Pick lemma

🔥 High
📖 LemmaFor \(|a|\lt1\), \(\varphi_a(z)=\dfrac{z-a}{1-\bar az}\) is a one-one map of \(D\) onto \(D\), \(\varphi_a^{-1}=\varphi_{-a}\), \(\varphi_a(a)=0\), \(\varphi_a'(0)=1-|a|^2\), \(\varphi_a'(a)=\dfrac1{1-|a|^2}\).
Proof: For \(|z|=1\): \(|1-\bar az|=|\bar z-\bar a|=|z-a|\) (since \(z\bar z=1\)), so \(|\varphi_a(z)|=1\). \(\varphi_a\) is analytic on \(|z|\lt1/|a|\), so by max modulus \(|\varphi_a|\lt1\) on \(D\) (non-constant). Direct check: \(\varphi_{-a}(\varphi_a(z))=z\), so onto. \(\varphi_a'(z)=\dfrac{1-|a|^2}{(1-\bar az)^2}\).
📜 Theorem (Schwarz–Pick)If \(f:D\to D\) is analytic, then for \(a\in D\), \(f(a)=\alpha\): $$|f'(a)|\le\frac{1-|f(a)|^2}{1-|a|^2}.$$ Equality holds iff \(f(z)=\varphi_{-\alpha}(c\varphi_a(z))\) with \(|c|=1\).
✒️ Proof Put \(g=\varphi_\alpha\circ f\circ\varphi_{-a}\). Then \(g:D\to D\) is analytic and \(g(0)=\varphi_\alpha(f(a))=\varphi_\alpha(\alpha)=0\). By Schwarz lemma \(|g'(0)|\le1\). By chain rule $$g'(0)=\varphi_\alpha'(\alpha)\,f'(a)\,\varphi_{-a}'(0)=\frac{1}{1-|\alpha|^2}\,f'(a)\,(1-|a|^2).$$ So \(|f'(a)|\le\dfrac{1-|\alpha|^2}{1-|a|^2}\). Equality ⇔ \(|g'(0)|=1\) ⇔ \(g(z)=cz\) ⇔ \(f=\varphi_{-\alpha}\circ(c\varphi_a)\). \(\blacksquare\)

T2.6 Automorphisms of the unit disk (application of Schwarz lemma)

🔥🔥 Very high
📜 TheoremLet \(f:D\to D\) be analytic, one-one and onto, with \(f(a)=0\). Then there is \(c\), \(|c|=1\), such that $$f(z)=c\,\varphi_a(z)=c\,\frac{z-a}{1-\bar az}.$$
✒️ Proof Let \(g=f^{-1}:D\to D\) (analytic, as inverse of a one-one analytic map). Put \(F=f\circ\varphi_{-a}\); then \(F:D\to D\) is analytic, one-one, onto, and \(F(0)=f(a)=0\). By Schwarz lemma \(|F(z)|\le|z|\). The inverse \(F^{-1}=\varphi_a\circ g\) also maps \(D\) onto \(D\) with \(F^{-1}(0)=0\), so again \(|F^{-1}(w)|\le|w|\). Put \(w=F(z)\): \(|z|\le|F(z)|\). Hence \(|F(z)|=|z|\) for all \(z\), and by the equality case of Schwarz lemma \(F(z)=cz\), \(|c|=1\). Therefore \(f(\varphi_{-a}(z))=cz\); replace \(z\) by \(\varphi_a(z)\): \(f(z)=c\varphi_a(z)\). \(\blacksquare\)
Corollary: the automorphisms of \(D\) with \(f(0)=0\) are exactly the rotations \(f(z)=cz\).

T2.7 Hadamard three circles theorem

🔥 High
📜 TheoremLet \(0\lt R_1\lt R_2\lt\infty\) and let \(f\) be analytic on the annulus \(R_1\le|z|\le R_2\) (non-zero function). For \(R_1\le r\le R_2\) let \(M(r)=\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|\). Then for \(R_1\le r\le R_2\): $$\log M(r)\le\frac{\log R_2-\log r}{\log R_2-\log R_1}\log M(R_1)+\frac{\log r-\log R_1}{\log R_2-\log R_1}\log M(R_2),$$ i.e. \(\log M(r)\) is a convex function of \(\log r\). Equivalently \(M(r)^{\log(R_2/R_1)}\le M(R_1)^{\log(R_2/r)}M(R_2)^{\log(r/R_1)}\).
✒️ Proof Let \(\alpha\) be any real number. Consider \(h(z)=|z|^\alpha|f(z)|\). Locally \(z^\alpha\) has an analytic branch and \(h=|z^\alpha f(z)|\) is the modulus of an analytic function; hence \(h\) cannot have a strict interior maximum unless it is locally constant, and the maximum of \(h\) on the closed annulus is attained on the boundary circles: $$r^\alpha M(r)\le\max\{R_1^\alpha M(R_1),\ R_2^\alpha M(R_2)\}.$$ Choose \(\alpha\) so that both are equal: \(R_1^\alpha M(R_1)=R_2^\alpha M(R_2)\), i.e. $$\alpha=\frac{\log M(R_1)-\log M(R_2)}{\log R_2-\log R_1}.$$ Then \(r^\alpha M(r)\le R_1^\alpha M(R_1)\), so \(\log M(r)\le\log M(R_1)+\alpha(\log R_1-\log r)\). Substituting \(\alpha\): $$\log M(r)\le\log M(R_1)-\frac{(\log r-\log R_1)(\log M(R_1)-\log M(R_2))}{\log R_2-\log R_1},$$ which rearranges to the stated inequality (coefficients of \(\log M(R_1)\) and \(\log M(R_2)\) are \(\frac{\log R_2-\log r}{\log R_2-\log R_1}\) and \(\frac{\log r-\log R_1}{\log R_2-\log R_1}\)). \(\blacksquare\)

T2.8 Hadamard three lines theorem

⭐ Medium
📜 TheoremLet \(S=\{x+iy: a\lt x\lt b\}\) and let \(f\) be continuous and bounded on \(\bar S\), analytic in \(S\). Let \(M(x)=\sup_y|f(x+iy)|\). Then $$M(x)^{\,b-a}\le M(a)^{\,b-x}\,M(b)^{\,x-a},\qquad a\lt x\lt b,$$ i.e. \(\log M(x)\) is a convex function of \(x\).
✒️ Proof Assume \(M(a),M(b)>0\) (if one is 0, replace it by \(\varepsilon>0\) and let \(\varepsilon\to0\)). Define $$g(z)=f(z)\,M(a)^{\frac{z-b}{b-a}}\,M(b)^{\frac{a-z}{b-a}}\qquad(M^{w}=e^{w\log M}).$$ Since \(|M^{w}|=M^{\operatorname{Re}w}\): \(|g(z)|=|f(z)|\,M(a)^{\frac{x-b}{b-a}}M(b)^{\frac{a-x}{b-a}}\). On \(x=a\): \(|g|=|f|/M(a)\le1\); on \(x=b\): \(|g|=|f|/M(b)\le1\). Also \(g\) is bounded on \(\bar S\), say \(|g|\le B\).
For \(\varepsilon>0\) put \(g_\varepsilon(z)=g(z)e^{\varepsilon z^2}\); \(|e^{\varepsilon z^2}|=e^{\varepsilon(x^2-y^2)}\). Let \(c=\max(|a|,|b|)\). On \(x=a\) or \(x=b\): \(|g_\varepsilon|\le e^{\varepsilon c^2}\). On \(|y|=Y\): \(|g_\varepsilon|\le Be^{\varepsilon(c^2-Y^2)}\le e^{\varepsilon c^2}\) for \(Y\) large. Apply the maximum modulus theorem on the rectangle \(a\le x\le b,\ |y|\le Y\): \(|g_\varepsilon(z)|\le e^{\varepsilon c^2}\) inside. Letting \(Y\to\infty\) then \(\varepsilon\to0\): \(|g(z)|\le1\) on \(S\). Therefore $$|f(x+iy)|\le M(a)^{\frac{b-x}{b-a}}M(b)^{\frac{x-a}{b-a}},$$ and taking sup over \(y\) gives the result. \(\blacksquare\)

T2.9 Borel–Carathéodory theorem

🔥 High
📜 TheoremLet \(f\) be analytic on \(|z|\le R\). Let \(M(r)=\max_{|z|=r}|f(z)|\), \(A(r)=\max_{|z|=r}\operatorname{Re}f(z)\). Then for \(0\lt r\lt R\): $$M(r)\le\frac{2r}{R-r}\,A(R)+\frac{R+r}{R-r}\,|f(0)| .$$
✒️ Proof Case 1: \(f(0)=0\). If \(f\) is constant, \(f\equiv0\) and the result is trivial. Otherwise \(A=A(R)>0\): indeed \(\operatorname{Re}f\) is harmonic with \(\operatorname{Re}f(0)=0\) equal to its mean over \(|z|=R\), so it takes positive values there. On \(|z|\le R\) we have \(\operatorname{Re}f\le A\) (max principle for harmonic functions). Define $$g(z)=\frac{f(z)}{2A-f(z)} .$$ \(\operatorname{Re}(2A-f)\ge2A-A=A>0\), so \(g\) is analytic on \(|z|\le R\), and \(g(0)=0\). Also $$|2A-f|^2-|f|^2=4A^2-4A\operatorname{Re}f\ge0\ \Rightarrow\ |g|\le1 .$$ Apply Schwarz lemma to \(g(Rz)\) on \(D\): \(|g(z)|\le\dfrac{|z|}{R}\). Solving, \(f=\dfrac{2Ag}{1+g}\), so on \(|z|=r\): $$|f(z)|\le\frac{2A|g|}{1-|g|}\le\frac{2A\,r/R}{1-r/R}=\frac{2r}{R-r}A(R).$$ Case 2 (general). Apply Case 1 to \(F=f-f(0)\): \(\max_{|z|=R}\operatorname{Re}F\le A(R)+|f(0)|\). So $$M(r)\le|f(0)|+\frac{2r}{R-r}\big(A(R)+|f(0)|\big)=\frac{2r}{R-r}A(R)+\frac{R+r}{R-r}|f(0)| .\ \blacksquare$$

T2.10 Phragmén–Lindelöf theorem (strip form)

⭐ Medium
📜 TheoremLet \(S=\{z: |\operatorname{Im}z|\lt\pi/2\}\), \(f\) continuous on \(\bar S\), analytic in \(S\), with \(|f|\le M\) on \(\partial S\). Suppose there are constants \(A\lt\infty\) and \(\alpha\lt1\) with $$|f(x+iy)|\le\exp\big(A\,e^{\alpha|x|}\big)\quad(z\in S).$$ Then \(|f(z)|\le M\) for all \(z\in S\).
✒️ Proof Choose \(\beta\) with \(\alpha\lt\beta\lt1\). For \(\varepsilon>0\) define \(h_\varepsilon(z)=f(z)\exp(-\varepsilon\cosh\beta z)\). For \(z=x+iy\in\bar S\): $$\operatorname{Re}\cosh\beta z=\cosh\beta x\cos\beta y\ge\delta\cosh\beta x,\quad\delta=\cos\tfrac{\beta\pi}{2}>0 .$$ So \(|h_\varepsilon(z)|\le|f(z)|\le M\) on \(\partial S\), and inside $$|h_\varepsilon(z)|\le\exp\big(Ae^{\alpha|x|}-\varepsilon\delta\cosh\beta x\big)\to0\ \text{ as }|x|\to\infty,$$ because \(\beta>\alpha\). Hence for \(X\) large \(|h_\varepsilon|\le M\) on the vertical sides \(x=\pm X\) of the rectangle \(R_X=\{|x|\le X,|y|\le\pi/2\}\), and \(\le M\) on the horizontal sides. By max modulus \(|h_\varepsilon|\le M\) in \(R_X\), hence in \(S\). Letting \(\varepsilon\to0\): \(|f(z)|\le M\). \(\blacksquare\)
Idea (yaad rakhne ke liye): unbounded region me max modulus seedha nahi lagta; ek "damping factor" se multiply karke bounded rectangle par lagate hain, phir \(\varepsilon\to0\).

T2.11 Liouville's theorem and Fundamental theorem of algebra

⭐ Medium
📜 Theorem(Liouville) A bounded entire function is constant.
(FTA) Every non-constant polynomial \(p(z)\) has a zero in \(\mathbb C\); hence a polynomial of degree \(n\) has exactly \(n\) zeros counted with multiplicity.
✒️ Proof Liouville: If \(|f|\le M\), Cauchy's estimate on \(|z-a|=R\) gives \(|f'(a)|\le M/R\to0\) as \(R\to\infty\). So \(f'\equiv0\), \(f\) constant.
FTA: Let \(p(z)=a_nz^n+\dots+a_0\), \(n\ge1\), \(a_n\ne0\), and suppose \(p(z)\ne0\) for all \(z\). Then \(f=1/p\) is entire. Since \(|p(z)|=|z|^n|a_n+a_{n-1}/z+\dots+a_0/z^n|\to\infty\) as \(|z|\to\infty\), \(f(z)\to0\); so \(f\) is bounded (continuous on a large disk, small outside). By Liouville \(f\), hence \(p\), is constant — contradiction. So \(p(z_1)=0\); then \(p(z)=(z-z_1)p_1(z)\), \(\deg p_1=n-1\), and induction gives \(n\) zeros. \(\blacksquare\)

T3.1 Riemann's removable singularity theorem

🔥 High
📖 Definition\(a\) is an isolated singularity of \(f\) if \(f\) is analytic in \(0\lt|z-a|\lt R\) but not at \(a\). It is removable if there is an analytic \(g\) on \(|z-a|\lt R\) with \(g=f\) for \(0\lt|z-a|\lt R\); a pole if \(|f(z)|\to\infty\) as \(z\to a\); essential otherwise.
📜 TheoremIf \(f\) has an isolated singularity at \(a\), then \(a\) is removable iff \(\displaystyle\lim_{z\to a}(z-a)f(z)=0\). (In particular, if \(f\) is bounded near \(a\).)
✒️ Proof If removable, \(f\to g(a)\) so \((z-a)f(z)\to0\).
Conversely, suppose \((z-a)f(z)\to0\). Define \(h(z)=(z-a)^2f(z)\) for \(z\ne a\), \(h(a)=0\). Then $$\frac{h(z)-h(a)}{z-a}=(z-a)f(z)\to0,$$ so \(h\) is differentiable at \(a\) with \(h'(a)=0\); \(h\) is analytic in the punctured disk, hence analytic on \(B(a;R)\). Its Taylor series: \(h(z)=\sum_{n\ge2}c_n(z-a)^n\) (since \(h(a)=h'(a)=0\)). So \(h(z)=(z-a)^2g(z)\), \(g(z)=\sum_{n\ge2}c_n(z-a)^{n-2}\) analytic, and \(g=f\) for \(z\ne a\). Hence \(a\) is removable. \(\blacksquare\)

T3.2 Laurent's theorem

🔥🔥 Very high
📜 TheoremLet \(f\) be analytic in the annulus \(\operatorname{ann}(a;R_1,R_2)=\{R_1\lt|z-a|\lt R_2\}\). Then $$f(z)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}a_n(z-a)^n,$$ the series converging absolutely and uniformly on every closed sub-annulus \(r_1\le|z-a|\le r_2\) (\(R_1\lt r_1\lt r_2\lt R_2\)), where $$a_n=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{|\zeta-a|=r}\frac{f(\zeta)}{(\zeta-a)^{n+1}}\,d\zeta\qquad(R_1\lt r\lt R_2),$$ and the coefficients are unique.
✒️ Proof Step 1 (coefficients independent of \(r\)). \(f(\zeta)/(\zeta-a)^{n+1}\) is analytic in the annulus; the circles \(|\zeta-a|=r\) and \(|\zeta-a|=r'\) are homotopic there, so by Cauchy's theorem the integrals are equal.
Step 2 (Cauchy formula for annulus). Fix \(z\) and choose \(R_1\lt r_1\lt|z-a|\lt r_2\lt R_2\). Let \(\gamma_1:|\zeta-a|=r_1\), \(\gamma_2:|\zeta-a|=r_2\) (anticlockwise). Applying Cauchy's integral formula to the cycle \(\gamma_2-\gamma_1\) (winding number 1 about \(z\), 0 about points outside the annulus): $$f(z)=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{\gamma_2}\frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\zeta-\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{\gamma_1}\frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\zeta .$$ Step 3 (outer integral). On \(\gamma_2\), \(|z-a|\lt|\zeta-a|\): $$\frac1{\zeta-z}=\frac1{(\zeta-a)\big(1-\frac{z-a}{\zeta-a}\big)}=\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{(z-a)^n}{(\zeta-a)^{n+1}},$$ uniformly convergent in \(\zeta\in\gamma_2\) (ratio \(|z-a|/r_2\lt1\)). Integrating termwise: first integral \(=\sum_{n\ge0}a_n(z-a)^n\).
Step 4 (inner integral). On \(\gamma_1\), \(|\zeta-a|\lt|z-a|\): $$-\frac1{\zeta-z}=\frac1{(z-a)\big(1-\frac{\zeta-a}{z-a}\big)}=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{(\zeta-a)^{n-1}}{(z-a)^{n}},$$ uniformly in \(\zeta\in\gamma_1\). So the second term \(=\sum_{n\ge1}\Big(\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{\gamma_1}f(\zeta)(\zeta-a)^{n-1}d\zeta\Big)(z-a)^{-n}=\sum_{n\ge1}a_{-n}(z-a)^{-n}\).
Adding, \(f(z)=\sum_{-\infty}^{\infty}a_n(z-a)^n\). The two series are a power series in \((z-a)\) with radius \(\ge R_2\) and a power series in \(1/(z-a)\) converging for \(|z-a|>R_1\), so convergence is absolute and uniform on closed sub-annuli.
Step 5 (uniqueness). If \(f=\sum b_n(z-a)^n\) on the annulus (uniformly on \(|z-a|=r\)), multiply by \((z-a)^{-k-1}\) and integrate termwise on \(|z-a|=r\): since \(\oint(z-a)^{m}dz=2\pi i\) for \(m=-1\) and 0 otherwise, we get \(\oint f(z)(z-a)^{-k-1}dz=2\pi i\,b_k\), i.e. \(b_k=a_k\). \(\blacksquare\)

T3.3 Nature of singularity from Laurent series; singularity at ∞; meromorphic ⇒ rational

🔥 High
📜 TheoremLet \(f=\sum_{-\infty}^\infty a_n(z-a)^n\) in \(0\lt|z-a|\lt R\). Then \(a\) is
(a) removable iff \(a_n=0\) for all \(n\le-1\);
(b) a pole of order \(m\) iff \(a_{-m}\ne0\) and \(a_n=0\) for \(n\le-(m+1)\);
(c) essential iff \(a_n\ne0\) for infinitely many negative \(n\).
At ∞: \(f\) has a removable singularity / pole / essential singularity at \(\infty\) according as \(f(1/z)\) has one at 0. An entire function with a pole of order \(m\) at \(\infty\) is a polynomial of degree \(m\). A function meromorphic on \(\hat{\mathbb C}\) is rational.
✒️ Proof (a) If no negative terms, the series is a power series: removable. Conversely if removable, \(f\) is bounded near \(a\), \(|f|\le M\); then \(|a_{-n}|=\left|\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_{|z-a|=r}f(z)(z-a)^{n-1}dz\right|\le Mr^n\to0\) as \(r\to0\), so \(a_{-n}=0\).
(b) \(a\) is a pole of order \(m\) iff \((z-a)^mf(z)\) has a removable singularity with non-zero value at \(a\); its Laurent series is \(\sum a_n(z-a)^{n+m}\). By (a) this happens iff \(a_n=0\) for \(n+m\le-1\) and the constant term \(a_{-m}\ne0\).
(c) follows from (a), (b) by elimination.
Entire with pole at ∞: \(f(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}a_nz^n\) for all \(z\), so \(f(1/z)=\sum a_nz^{-n}\); a pole of order \(m\) at 0 means \(a_n=0\) for \(n>m\), \(a_m\ne0\): a polynomial of degree \(m\).
Meromorphic on \(\hat{\mathbb C}\) ⇒ rational: poles are isolated and \(\hat{\mathbb C}\) is compact, so there are finitely many poles \(b_1,\dots,b_k\) in \(\mathbb C\) (and possibly \(\infty\)). Let \(P_j\) be the principal part at \(b_j\) (a polynomial in \(1/(z-b_j)\)) and \(Q\) the principal part at \(\infty\) (a polynomial). Then \(g=f-\sum P_j-Q\) is entire and bounded at \(\infty\) (removable), so by Liouville \(g\) is constant. Hence \(f\) is rational. \(\blacksquare\)

T3.4 Argument principle

🔥🔥 Very high
📜 TheoremLet \(f\) be meromorphic in a region \(G\) with zeros \(z_1,\dots,z_n\) and poles \(p_1,\dots,p_m\) (repeated according to multiplicity) inside a simple closed rectifiable curve \(\gamma\subset G\) (with \(\gamma\approx0\) in \(G\)), and no zeros or poles on \(\gamma\). Then $$\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma\frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}\,dz=N-P,$$ where \(N\) = number of zeros and \(P\) = number of poles inside \(\gamma\). (Also \(=\frac1{2\pi}\Delta_\gamma\arg f(z)\), the change of argument.)
✒️ Proof Repeatedly factor out zeros and poles: $$f(z)=\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{n}(z-z_k)}{\prod_{j=1}^{m}(z-p_j)}\,g(z),$$ where \(g\) is analytic and never zero inside and on \(\gamma\) (in a neighbourhood). Taking the logarithmic derivative (valid where \(f\ne0,\infty\)): $$\frac{f'(z)}{f(z)}=\sum_{k=1}^n\frac1{z-z_k}-\sum_{j=1}^m\frac1{z-p_j}+\frac{g'(z)}{g(z)} .$$ \(g'/g\) is analytic inside and on \(\gamma\), so \(\oint_\gamma g'/g=0\) (Cauchy). And \(\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma\frac{dz}{z-c}=1\) for \(c\) inside \(\gamma\). Therefore $$\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma\frac{f'}{f}dz=n-m=N-P.$$ Finally, \(\frac{f'}{f}=\frac{d}{dz}\log f\), so the integral equals \(\frac1{2\pi i}[\log|f|+i\arg f]_\gamma=\frac1{2\pi}\Delta_\gamma\arg f\) (\(\log|f|\) returns to its value). \(\blacksquare\)

T3.5 Rouché's theorem

🔥🔥 Very high
📜 TheoremLet \(f,g\) be analytic inside and on a simple closed contour \(\gamma\), and suppose \(|g(z)|\lt|f(z)|\) on \(\gamma\). Then \(f\) and \(f+g\) have the same number of zeros inside \(\gamma\) (counted with multiplicity).
✒️ Proof On \(\gamma\), \(|f|>|g|\ge0\), so \(f\ne0\) on \(\gamma\). For \(0\le t\le1\) put \(F_t=f+tg\). On \(\gamma\): $$|F_t(z)|\ge|f(z)|-t|g(z)|\ge|f(z)|-|g(z)|>0,$$ so no \(F_t\) vanishes on \(\gamma\). By the argument principle the number of zeros of \(F_t\) inside \(\gamma\) is $$N(t)=\frac1{2\pi i}\oint_\gamma\frac{f'(z)+tg'(z)}{f(z)+tg(z)}\,dz .$$ The integrand is a continuous function of \((t,z)\in[0,1]\times\gamma\) (denominator bounded away from 0 on this compact set), so \(N(t)\) is continuous in \(t\). But \(N(t)\) is an integer for every \(t\). A continuous integer-valued function on \([0,1]\) is constant. Hence \(N(0)=N(1)\): \(f\) and \(f+g\) have the same number of zeros inside \(\gamma\). \(\blacksquare\)

T3.6 Casorati–Weierstrass theorem

🔥🔥 Very high
📜 TheoremIf \(f\) has an essential singularity at \(z=a\), then for every \(\delta>0\), \(f\big(\{0\lt|z-a|\lt\delta\}\big)\) is dense in \(\mathbb C\). (Equivalently: for every \(w\in\mathbb C\) there is a sequence \(z_n\to a\) with \(f(z_n)\to w\).)
✒️ Proof Suppose not. Then there are \(\delta>0\), \(w\in\mathbb C\), \(\varepsilon>0\) with $$|f(z)-w|\ge\varepsilon\quad\text{for }0\lt|z-a|\lt\delta .$$ Define \(g(z)=\dfrac1{f(z)-w}\). Then \(g\) is analytic in the punctured disk and \(|g|\le1/\varepsilon\). By Riemann's theorem (T3.1), \(a\) is a removable singularity of \(g\).
If \(g(a)\ne0\), then \(f(z)=w+1/g(z)\) is analytic at \(a\): removable singularity of \(f\).
If \(g(a)=0\) with a zero of order \(m\), then \(f=w+1/g\) has a pole of order \(m\) at \(a\).
Either way \(a\) is not essential — contradiction. \(\blacksquare\)

T4.1 Schwarz reflection principle

🔥 High
📜 TheoremLet \(G\) be a region symmetric about the real axis (\(z\in G\Rightarrow\bar z\in G\)). Let \(G_+=\{z\in G:\operatorname{Im}z>0\}\), \(G_0=G\cap\mathbb R\), \(G_-=\{z\in G:\operatorname{Im}z\lt0\}\). If \(f:G_+\cup G_0\to\mathbb C\) is continuous, analytic in \(G_+\) and real on \(G_0\), then $$F(z)=\begin{cases}f(z),& z\in G_+\cup G_0\\ \overline{f(\bar z)},& z\in G_-\end{cases}$$ is analytic on \(G\).
✒️ Proof (1) \(F\) is analytic on \(G_-\). For \(z_0\in G_-\), \(\bar z_0\in G_+\) and near \(\bar z_0\), \(f(\zeta)=\sum a_n(\zeta-\bar z_0)^n\). Then for \(z\) near \(z_0\): $$F(z)=\overline{f(\bar z)}=\overline{\sum a_n(\bar z-\bar z_0)^n}=\sum\bar a_n(z-z_0)^n,$$ a power series. So \(F\) is analytic in \(G_-\).
(2) \(F\) is continuous on \(G\). On \(G_0\), \(f\) is real, so \(\overline{f(\bar x)}=f(x)\); with continuity of \(f\) on \(G_+\cup G_0\) this shows \(F\) is continuous across \(G_0\).
(3) Morera. Let \(x_0\in G_0\) and \(B=B(x_0;R)\subset G\). It is enough to show \(\int_T F=0\) for every triangle \(T\subset B\). If \(T\) lies in \(\bar G_+\cap B\) with one side on the real axis, let \(T_\varepsilon\) be the triangle obtained by moving that side up to \(\operatorname{Im}z=\varepsilon\); \(\int_{T_\varepsilon}F=0\) by Cauchy (Goursat) since \(T_\varepsilon\subset G_+\). As \(\varepsilon\to0\), uniform continuity of \(F\) on compact sets gives \(\int_TF=\lim\int_{T_\varepsilon}F=0\). Same in the lower half. A general triangle meeting the real axis is split by the axis into triangles (and quadrilaterals = two triangles) each of the above type, so \(\int_TF=0\). By Morera's theorem \(F\) is analytic in \(B\). Hence \(F\) is analytic on \(G\). \(\blacksquare\)

T4.2 Mittag-Leffler theorem

🔥 High
📜 TheoremLet \(\{a_n\}\) be a sequence of distinct complex numbers with \(|a_1|\le|a_2|\le\dots\), \(|a_n|\to\infty\), and let \(P_n(z)\) be polynomials without constant term. Then there is a function \(f\), meromorphic in \(\mathbb C\), whose poles are exactly the points \(a_n\), with principal part \(P_n\!\left(\dfrac1{z-a_n}\right)\) at \(a_n\). The most general such function is $$f(z)=\sum_n\left[P_n\!\left(\frac1{z-a_n}\right)-Q_n(z)\right]+g(z),$$ with \(Q_n\) suitable polynomials and \(g\) entire.
✒️ Proof If \(a_1=0\) take its term separately (no correction); so assume \(a_n\ne0\). The function \(h_n(z)=P_n\!\left(\frac1{z-a_n}\right)\) is analytic in \(|z|\lt|a_n|\), so its Taylor series about 0 converges uniformly on \(|z|\le|a_n|/2\). Choose a partial sum \(Q_n(z)\) (a polynomial) with $$|h_n(z)-Q_n(z)|\lt2^{-n}\quad\text{for }|z|\le\tfrac{|a_n|}{2}.$$ Claim: \(f=\sum_n(h_n-Q_n)\) is meromorphic with the required poles. Fix \(R>0\). Since \(|a_n|\to\infty\), there is \(N\) with \(|a_n|\ge2R\) for \(n>N\). For \(|z|\le R\) and \(n>N\): \(|z|\le|a_n|/2\), so \(|h_n-Q_n|\lt2^{-n}\). By the Weierstrass M-test, \(\sum_{n>N}(h_n-Q_n)\) converges uniformly on \(|z|\le R\) to an analytic function. The finite sum \(\sum_{n\le N}(h_n-Q_n)\) is meromorphic in \(|z|\lt R\) with poles exactly the \(a_n\) (\(n\le N\)) there and principal parts \(h_n\). As \(R\) is arbitrary, \(f\) is meromorphic in \(\mathbb C\) with the given poles and principal parts.
If \(F\) is another such function, \(F-f\) has only removable singularities, so \(F=f+g\), \(g\) entire. \(\blacksquare\)
✍️ Example\(\pi\cot\pi z=\dfrac1z+\displaystyle\sum_{n\ne0}\left(\frac1{z-n}+\frac1n\right)\): poles at integers with principal part \(\frac1{z-n}\), correction \(Q_n=-\frac1n\).

T4.3 Convergence of infinite products

🔥 High
📖 Definition\(\prod_{n=1}^\infty(1+u_n)\) converges if at most finitely many factors are 0 and, after removing them, the partial products tend to a non-zero limit. It converges absolutely if \(\prod(1+|u_n|)\) converges.
📜 Theorem(a) If \(\operatorname{Re}u_n>-1\), then \(\prod(1+u_n)\) converges iff \(\sum\log(1+u_n)\) converges (principal log).
(b) If \(\sum|u_n|\lt\infty\), then \(\prod(1+u_n)\) converges (absolutely).
(c) If \(u_n\) are analytic on \(G\) and \(\sum|u_n(z)|\) converges uniformly on compact subsets of \(G\), then \(f=\prod(1+u_n(z))\) converges uniformly on compacta to an analytic function, and \(f(z)=0\) iff some \(1+u_n(z)=0\).
✒️ Proof (a) Let \(p_n=\prod_{k\le n}(1+u_k)\), \(s_n=\sum_{k\le n}\log(1+u_k)\); \(p_n=e^{s_n}\). If \(s_n\to s\), then \(p_n\to e^s\ne0\). Conversely if \(p_n\to p\ne0\), one shows \(s_n\) converges: \(s_n=\log p_n+2\pi ik_n\) with integers \(k_n\); \(s_{n+1}-s_n=\log(1+u_{n+1})\to0\) (since \(u_n\to0\)), and \(\log p_{n+1}-\log p_n\to0\) for a branch near \(p\), so \(k_{n+1}-k_n\to0\); being integers, \(k_n\) is eventually constant and \(s_n\) converges.
(b) Since \(u_n\to0\), \(|u_n|\le\frac12\) for \(n\ge N\). For \(|u|\le\frac12\): $$|\log(1+u)|=\Big|u-\frac{u^2}{2}+\frac{u^3}{3}-\dots\Big|\le|u|\big(1+|u|+|u|^2+\dots\big)=\frac{|u|}{1-|u|}\le2|u| .$$ So \(\sum_{n\ge N}|\log(1+u_n)|\le2\sum|u_n|\lt\infty\), and by (a) the product converges.
(c) On a compact \(K\), the same estimate gives uniform convergence of \(\sum_{n\ge N}\log(1+u_n(z))\) to an analytic \(S(z)\) (Weierstrass M-test), hence \(\prod_{n\ge N}(1+u_n)=e^{S}\ne0\) uniformly; multiplying by the finitely many first factors gives the result. \(\blacksquare\)
✍️ Example\(\prod_{n\ge2}\left(1-\frac1{n^2}\right)\): \(\sum\frac1{n^2}\lt\infty\) so it converges; partial product \(=\frac{N+1}{2N}\to\frac12\).

T4.4 Weierstrass elementary factors

🔥 High
📖 Definition\(E_0(z)=1-z\), \(E_p(z)=(1-z)\exp\!\left(z+\dfrac{z^2}{2}+\dots+\dfrac{z^p}{p}\right)\), \(p\ge1\).
📜 LemmaFor \(|z|\le1\) and \(p\ge0\): \(|1-E_p(z)|\le|z|^{p+1}\).
✒️ Proof For \(p=0\) it is an equality. Let \(p\ge1\). Differentiating, $$E_p'(z)=-z^p\exp\!\left(z+\frac{z^2}2+\dots+\frac{z^p}p\right).$$ So \(-E_p'(z)\) has a zero of order \(p\) at 0 and, expanding the exponential, its Taylor coefficients are all \(\ge0\). Since \(1-E_p(0)=0\), integrating: \(1-E_p(z)=\sum_{n\ge p+1}b_nz^n\) with \(b_n\ge0\). Put \(\varphi(z)=\dfrac{1-E_p(z)}{z^{p+1}}=\sum_{n\ge p+1}b_nz^{n-p-1}\). For \(|z|\le1\): $$|\varphi(z)|\le\sum b_n|z|^{n-p-1}\le\sum b_n=\varphi(1)=1-E_p(1)=1 .$$ Hence \(|1-E_p(z)|\le|z|^{p+1}\). \(\blacksquare\)

T4.5 Weierstrass factorization theorem

🔥🔥 Very high
📜 TheoremLet \(f\) be an entire function, not identically zero, with a zero of order \(m\ge0\) at \(z=0\), and let \(\{a_n\}\) be its other zeros (repeated by multiplicity, \(|a_n|\to\infty\)). Then there exist an entire function \(g\) and integers \(p_n\ge0\) such that $$f(z)=z^m e^{g(z)}\prod_{n=1}^\infty E_{p_n}\!\left(\frac{z}{a_n}\right).$$ (Conversely, for any sequence \(a_n\to\infty\) such a product defines an entire function with exactly these zeros.)
✒️ Proof Step 1 (product converges). Take \(p_n=n-1\). Fix \(R>0\). Since \(|a_n|\to\infty\), there is \(N\) with \(|a_n|\ge2R\) for \(n>N\). For \(|z|\le R\), \(n>N\): \(|z/a_n|\le\frac12\), so by T4.4 $$\Big|1-E_{p_n}\!\Big(\frac z{a_n}\Big)\Big|\le\Big|\frac z{a_n}\Big|^{n}\le\Big(\frac12\Big)^{n}.$$ So \(\sum_n|1-E_{p_n}(z/a_n)|\) converges uniformly on \(|z|\le R\). By T4.3(c), \(P(z)=\prod E_{p_n}(z/a_n)\) is entire, and its zeros are exactly the \(a_n\) (with multiplicity), because \(E_p(w)=0\) only at \(w=1\).
Step 2. Then \(h(z)=\dfrac{f(z)}{z^mP(z)}\) has only removable singularities, so it is entire and never zero.
Step 3. \(\mathbb C\) is simply connected and \(h\ne0\), so by T1.2(b) \(h=e^{g}\) for some entire \(g\). Hence \(f(z)=z^me^{g(z)}P(z)\). \(\blacksquare\)
✍️ Example\(\sin\pi z=\pi z\displaystyle\prod_{n=1}^\infty\left(1-\frac{z^2}{n^2}\right)\) (here \(p_n=1\) suffices since \(\sum1/n^2\lt\infty\), and pairing \(\pm n\) cancels the exponentials).

T4.6 Order, genus and Hadamard factorization theorem (statement)

⭐ Medium
📖 DefinitionsOrder of an entire \(f\): \(\lambda=\limsup_{r\to\infty}\dfrac{\log\log M(r)}{\log r}\), \(M(r)=\max_{|z|=r}|f|\).
Rank of zeros \(\{a_n\}\): least integer \(p\) with \(\sum|a_n|^{-(p+1)}\lt\infty\). The product \(\prod E_p(z/a_n)\) is then the canonical product.
Genus \(\mu\) of \(f=z^me^{g}\prod E_p(z/a_n)\) (canonical form, \(g\) polynomial): \(\mu=\max(p,\deg g)\).
📜 Theorem (Hadamard)If \(f\) is entire of finite order \(\lambda\), then \(f\) has finite genus \(\mu\le\lambda\), i.e. $$f(z)=z^m e^{g(z)}\prod_n E_p\!\left(\frac z{a_n}\right),$$ where \(g\) is a polynomial of degree \(\le\lambda\) and \(p\le\lambda\).
✍️ Example\(\sin z\): \(M(r)\approx e^r/2\), so \(\lambda=1\). Zeros \(n\pi\): \(\sum1/|n\pi|\) diverges, \(\sum1/(n\pi)^2\) converges ⇒ rank \(p=1\). Hadamard: \(\sin z=z e^{az+b}\prod_{n\ne0}(1-\frac z{n\pi})e^{z/n\pi}\); comparing (oddness, \(\sin z/z\to1\)) gives \(a=b=0\).
Also: \(e^{z}\) has order 1, \(e^{z^2}\) order 2, \(e^{e^z}\) infinite order, polynomials order 0.

10. Find the order of \(e^{z^2}\) and of \(\cos\sqrt z\). 2

Answer
Answer\(M(r)=e^{r^2}\) ⇒ \(\log\log M=2\log r\) ⇒ order 2. \(\cos\sqrt z=\sum\frac{(-1)^nz^n}{(2n)!}\) is entire, \(M(r)\approx\frac12e^{\sqrt r}\) ⇒ order \(\frac12\).

P1. Find the Möbius transformation which maps \(z=1,i,-1\) onto \(w=0,1,\infty\). 7½

Solution
Solution Since \(-1\mapsto\infty\) and \(1\mapsto0\): \(w=k\dfrac{z-1}{z+1}\). \(z=i\mapsto1\): \(1=k\dfrac{i-1}{i+1}=k\cdot\dfrac{(i-1)(1-i)}{2}=k\cdot\dfrac{2i}{2}=ki\Rightarrow k=-i\). $$w=-i\,\frac{z-1}{z+1}=\frac{i(1-z)}{1+z}.$$ Check: \(z=i\): \(\frac{i(1-i)}{1+i}=\frac{i(1-i)^2}{2}=\frac{i(-2i)}{2}=1\) ✓. (It maps the unit circle onto the real axis and \(|z|\lt1\) onto the upper half plane: \(z=0\mapsto i\).)

P2. Expand \(f(z)=\dfrac{1}{(z-1)(z-2)}\) in Laurent series in (i) \(|z|\lt1\), (ii) \(1\lt|z|\lt2\), (iii) \(|z|>2\), (iv) \(0\lt|z-1|\lt1\). 7½

Solution
Solution Partial fractions: \(f(z)=\dfrac1{z-2}-\dfrac1{z-1}\).
(i) \(|z|\lt1\): \(\frac1{z-2}=-\frac12\cdot\frac1{1-z/2}=-\sum\frac{z^n}{2^{n+1}}\); \(-\frac1{z-1}=\frac1{1-z}=\sum z^n\). $$f=\sum_{n=0}^\infty\Big(1-\frac1{2^{n+1}}\Big)z^n .$$ (ii) \(1\lt|z|\lt2\): \(-\frac1{z-1}=-\frac1z\cdot\frac1{1-1/z}=-\sum_{n\ge1}\frac1{z^n}\). $$f=-\sum_{n=0}^\infty\frac{z^n}{2^{n+1}}-\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac1{z^n}.$$ (iii) \(|z|>2\): \(\frac1{z-2}=\frac1z\cdot\frac1{1-2/z}=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{2^{n-1}}{z^n}\). $$f=\sum_{n=1}^\infty\frac{2^{n-1}-1}{z^n}=\frac1{z^2}+\frac3{z^3}+\frac7{z^4}+\dots$$ (iv) Put \(u=z-1\), \(0\lt|u|\lt1\): \(\frac1{z-2}=\frac1{u-1}=-\sum u^n\). $$f=-\frac1{z-1}-\sum_{n=0}^\infty(z-1)^n .$$ (simple pole at \(z=1\), residue \(-1\).)

P3. Find the Laurent series of \(e^{1/z}\) and \(z^2\sin\frac1z\) about \(z=0\) and state the nature of singularity. 3

Solution
Solution\(e^{1/z}=1+\frac1z+\frac1{2!z^2}+\dots\) (\(|z|>0\)); \(z^2\sin\frac1z=z-\frac1{3!z}+\frac1{5!z^3}-\dots\). Both have infinitely many negative powers ⇒ essential singularity at 0.

P4. Using Rouché's theorem, find the number of zeros of \(z^4-6z+3\) in \(|z|\lt1\) and in \(1\lt|z|\lt2\). 7½

Solution
Solution On \(|z|=1\): take \(f=-6z\), \(g=z^4+3\). \(|f|=6\), \(|g|\le1+3=4\lt6\). So \(z^4-6z+3\) has as many zeros in \(|z|\lt1\) as \(-6z\), i.e. 1.
On \(|z|=2\): take \(f=z^4\), \(g=-6z+3\). \(|f|=16\), \(|g|\le12+3=15\lt16\). So there are 4 zeros in \(|z|\lt2\).
No zero on \(|z|=1\) (since \(|z^4-6z+3|\ge6-4>0\)). Hence the number in \(1\lt|z|\lt2\) is \(4-1=\mathbf 3\).

P5. Show that all roots of \(z^7-5z^3+12=0\) lie between the circles \(|z|=1\) and \(|z|=2\). 7½

Solution
Solution On \(|z|=1\): \(f=12\), \(g=z^7-5z^3\), \(|g|\le1+5=6\lt12\) ⇒ same zeros as constant 12 ⇒ no zero in \(|z|\lt1\) (and none on \(|z|=1\)).
On \(|z|=2\): \(f=z^7\), \(|f|=128\); \(g=-5z^3+12\), \(|g|\le40+12=52\lt128\) ⇒ all 7 zeros in \(|z|\lt2\). Hence all 7 roots lie in \(1\lt|z|\lt2\).

P6. If \(f\) is analytic in \(|z|\lt1\), \(|f|\le1\), \(f(0)=0\), show \(|f(1/2)|\le\frac12\) and find all such \(f\) with \(|f'(0)|=1\). 3

Solution
SolutionBy Schwarz lemma \(|f(z)|\le|z|\), so \(|f(1/2)|\le\frac12\). \(|f'(0)|=1\) ⇒ \(f(z)=e^{i\theta}z\).

P7. Find the Möbius map of \(|z|\lt1\) onto \(|w|\lt1\) with \(f(\frac12)=0\) and \(f'(\frac12)>0\). 3

Solution
