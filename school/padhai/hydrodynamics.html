Unit I — Kinematics and Equations of Motion

Lagrangian & Eulerian methods, continuity, boundary surface, stream/path/streak lines, velocity potential, vorticity, Euler's and Lagrange's equations, Bernoulli, impulsive action.

1.1 Lagrangian and Eulerian methods

🔥 High — short question
💡 ConceptLagrangian: ek particle ko pakad kar uske saath chalo (nadi me patta). Eulerian: ek fixed jagah (pul) par khade hokar dekho ki wahan se guzarne wale paani ki velocity kya hai.
📖 Definitions Lagrangian method: the position of a particle is given as \(\mathbf r=\mathbf r(a,b,c,t)\), where (a, b, c) is its initial position; velocity \(\dot{\mathbf r}\), acceleration \(\ddot{\mathbf r}\).
Eulerian method: the velocity \(\mathbf q(x,y,z,t)\), pressure and density are studied at fixed points (x, y, z) of space as functions of time.
LagrangianEulerian
Independent variables a, b, c, tIndependent variables x, y, z, t
History of each particleField of flow at each point
Mathematically harderEasier, generally used
📜 Material (total) derivativeRate of change following a particle: $$\frac{D}{Dt}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+u\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+v\frac{\partial}{\partial y}+w\frac{\partial}{\partial z}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\mathbf q\cdot\nabla .$$
✒️ DerivationLet \(f(x,y,z,t)\). In time δt the particle moves from (x, y, z) to (x + uδt, y + vδt, z + wδt). So \(\delta f=\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\delta t+\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}u\delta t+\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}v\delta t+\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}w\delta t\). Divide by δt and let δt → 0. ∎ Acceleration \(=\frac{D\mathbf q}{Dt}=\frac{\partial\mathbf q}{\partial t}+(\mathbf q\cdot\nabla)\mathbf q\).

1.2 Equation of continuity

🔥 High — 10-mark derivation
💡 ConceptMass na banta hai na mitta hai. Kisi fixed volume me mass badhne ki rate = andar aane wala mass − bahar jaane wala mass.
📜 Eulerian form $$\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf q)=0,\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad\frac{D\rho}{Dt}+\rho\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf q=0 .$$
✒️ Derivation (vector method)
  1. Take an arbitrary closed surface S fixed in space enclosing volume V. Mass inside \(=\int_V\rho\,dV\).
  2. Rate of increase of mass inside \(=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_V\rho\,dV=\int_V\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}\,dV\).
  3. Rate of outflow of mass across S \(=\int_S\rho\,\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf n\,dS=\int_V\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf q)\,dV\) (Gauss divergence theorem; n = outward normal).
  4. No sources or sinks inside, so increase = −outflow: \(\int_V\big[\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf q)\big]dV=0\).
  5. This holds for every V and the integrand is continuous, so \(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf q)=0\). ∎
  6. Since \(\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf q)=\rho\nabla\cdot\mathbf q+\mathbf q\cdot\nabla\rho\), this is \(\frac{D\rho}{Dt}+\rho\nabla\cdot\mathbf q=0\).
✒️ Derivation (Cartesian, elementary parallelepiped)
  1. Take a small box with centre P(x, y, z) and edges δx, δy, δz parallel to the axes.
  2. Mass flowing in per unit time through the face at \(x-\frac{\delta x}{2}\): \(\big[\rho u-\frac{\delta x}{2}\frac{\partial(\rho u)}{\partial x}\big]\delta y\,\delta z\); out through the face at \(x+\frac{\delta x}{2}\): \(\big[\rho u+\frac{\delta x}{2}\frac{\partial(\rho u)}{\partial x}\big]\delta y\,\delta z\).
  3. Net outflow in the x-direction \(=\frac{\partial(\rho u)}{\partial x}\delta x\,\delta y\,\delta z\); similarly for y and z.
  4. Rate of increase of mass in the box \(=\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}\delta x\,\delta y\,\delta z\) = −(total net outflow).
  5. Dividing by δx δy δz: \(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial(\rho u)}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial(\rho v)}{\partial y}+\frac{\partial(\rho w)}{\partial z}=0\). ∎
📜 Lagrangian form\(\rho J=\rho_0\), where \(J=\dfrac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(a,b,c)}\) and \(\rho_0\) is the initial density.
✒️ DerivationA small element initially of volume δa δb δc (density \(\rho_0\)) occupies at time t the volume \(\delta x\,\delta y\,\delta z=J\,\delta a\,\delta b\,\delta c\). Its mass does not change: \(\rho J\,\delta a\,\delta b\,\delta c=\rho_0\,\delta a\,\delta b\,\delta c\), so \(\rho J=\rho_0\). ∎
💡 Special casesIncompressible (ρ constant): \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf q=0\), i.e. \(u_x+v_y+w_z=0\). Incompressible and irrotational (\(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\)): \(\nabla^2\phi=0\) (Laplace's equation).
✍️ Solved example Q. Show that \(u=-\frac{2xyz}{(x^2+y^2)^2},\ v=\frac{(x^2-y^2)z}{(x^2+y^2)^2},\ w=\frac{y}{x^2+y^2}\) are the velocity components of a possible liquid motion.
Let \(r^2=x^2+y^2\). \(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}=-2yz\,\frac{r^4-x\cdot2r^2\cdot2x}{r^8}=-2yz\,\frac{y^2-3x^2}{r^6}\). \(\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}=z\,\frac{-2y\,r^4-(x^2-y^2)\cdot2r^2\cdot2y}{r^8}=z\,\frac{-2y(x^2+y^2)-4y(x^2-y^2)}{r^6}=2yz\,\frac{y^2-3x^2}{r^6}\). \(\frac{\partial w}{\partial z}=0\). Sum \(=0\). The equation of continuity for an incompressible liquid holds, so the motion is possible. ∎

1.3 Boundary surface

⭐ Medium
📜 ResultA surface \(F(x,y,z,t)=0\) can be a boundary surface of a liquid iff $$\frac{DF}{Dt}\equiv\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}+u\frac{\partial F}{\partial x}+v\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}+w\frac{\partial F}{\partial z}=0\quad\text{on }F=0.$$
✒️ Derivation
  1. At a boundary there is no flow across it: the liquid velocity normal to the surface equals the normal velocity of the surface itself.
  2. Let a particle at (x, y, z) on the surface at time t move to (x + uδt, y + vδt, z + wδt) at t + δt. It stays on the surface, so \(F(x+u\delta t,\,y+v\delta t,\,z+w\delta t,\,t+\delta t)=0\).
  3. Expanding by Taylor's theorem and using F(x, y, z, t) = 0: \(\delta t\big[\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}+u\frac{\partial F}{\partial x}+v\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}+w\frac{\partial F}{\partial z}\big]+O(\delta t^2)=0\).
  4. Divide by δt and let δt → 0: \(\frac{DF}{Dt}=0\). ∎ (Equivalently, normal velocity of the surface \(=-\frac{\partial F/\partial t}{|\nabla F|}=\mathbf q\cdot\frac{\nabla F}{|\nabla F|}\).)
✍️ Solved example Q. Show that \(\frac{x^2}{a^2k^2t^4}+kt^2\left(\frac{y^2}{b^2}+\frac{z^2}{c^2}\right)=1\) is a possible form of boundary surface of a liquid, and find suitable velocity components.
Take \(F=\frac{x^2}{a^2k^2t^4}+kt^2\left(\frac{y^2}{b^2}+\frac{z^2}{c^2}\right)-1\). Try \(u=\frac{2x}{t},\ v=-\frac yt,\ w=-\frac zt\).
\(\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}=-\frac{4x^2}{a^2k^2t^5}+2kt\left(\frac{y^2}{b^2}+\frac{z^2}{c^2}\right)\); \(u\frac{\partial F}{\partial x}=\frac{2x}{t}\cdot\frac{2x}{a^2k^2t^4}=\frac{4x^2}{a^2k^2t^5}\); \(v\frac{\partial F}{\partial y}=-\frac yt\cdot\frac{2kt^2y}{b^2}=-\frac{2kty^2}{b^2}\); \(w\frac{\partial F}{\partial z}=-\frac{2ktz^2}{c^2}\). Sum \(=0\), so DF/Dt = 0. Also \(u_x+v_y+w_z=\frac2t-\frac1t-\frac1t=0\) (continuity holds). Hence F = 0 is a possible boundary surface with these velocities. ∎

1.4 Streamlines, pathlines and streak lines

🔥 High
📖 Definitions Streamline: a curve, drawn at a given instant, whose tangent at every point is in the direction of the velocity there.
Pathline: the curve actually traced by a single particle during its motion.
Streak line: the locus at a given instant of all particles that have passed through a fixed point (e.g. dye from a fixed nozzle).
In steady motion all three coincide.
📜 EquationsStreamlines: \(\dfrac{dx}{u}=\dfrac{dy}{v}=\dfrac{dz}{w}\) (t held constant). Pathlines: \(\dfrac{dx}{dt}=u,\ \dfrac{dy}{dt}=v,\ \dfrac{dz}{dt}=w\).
✒️ Derivation (streamline)Along a streamline the tangent \(d\mathbf r=(dx,dy,dz)\) is parallel to \(\mathbf q\): \(d\mathbf r\times\mathbf q=\mathbf 0\), which gives \(\frac{dx}u=\frac{dy}v=\frac{dz}w\). ∎
✍️ Solved example Q. For \(u=x,\ v=-y\) (2-D), find streamlines and pathlines.
Streamlines: \(\frac{dx}{x}=\frac{dy}{-y}\Rightarrow\log x=-\log y+\log c\Rightarrow\boxed{xy=c}\) (rectangular hyperbolas).
Pathlines: \(\frac{dx}{dt}=x\Rightarrow x=x_0e^t\); \(\frac{dy}{dt}=-y\Rightarrow y=y_0e^{-t}\). Eliminating t: \(xy=x_0y_0\) — the same hyperbolas (steady motion). ∎

1.5 Velocity potential, vorticity, rotational and irrotational motion

🔥 High
💡 ConceptPaani me chhoti chakri (paddle wheel) rakho: ghoome ⇒ rotational, na ghoome ⇒ irrotational. Ghoomne ka naap = vorticity.
📖 Definitions Vorticity \(\boldsymbol\omega=\nabla\times\mathbf q=(\xi,\eta,\zeta)\), \(\xi=w_y-v_z,\ \eta=u_z-w_x,\ \zeta=v_x-u_y\).
Motion is irrotational if \(\boldsymbol\omega=\mathbf 0\), otherwise rotational.
If \(u\,dx+v\,dy+w\,dz=-d\phi\) (exact), φ is the velocity potential: \(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\).
Vortex line: a curve whose tangent is along ω: \(\frac{dx}{\xi}=\frac{dy}{\eta}=\frac{dz}{\zeta}\). Vortex tube: vortex lines through every point of a small closed curve.
📜 TheoremThe motion is irrotational iff a velocity potential exists.
✒️ ProofIf \(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\), then \(\nabla\times\mathbf q=-\nabla\times\nabla\phi=\mathbf 0\). Conversely if \(\nabla\times\mathbf q=\mathbf 0\) in a simply connected region, \(u\,dx+v\,dy+w\,dz\) is an exact differential (conditions \(w_y=v_z\) etc.), so it equals \(-d\phi\) for some φ. ∎
✍️ Solved example Q. Show that \(u=y+z,\ v=z+x,\ w=x+y\) is irrotational and find φ. Is it a possible incompressible motion?
\(\xi=w_y-v_z=1-1=0\), \(\eta=u_z-w_x=1-1=0\), \(\zeta=v_x-u_y=1-1=0\): irrotational.
\(-d\phi=(y+z)dx+(z+x)dy+(x+y)dz=d(xy+yz+zx)\), so \(\boxed{\phi=-(xy+yz+zx)}\).
\(u_x+v_y+w_z=0\): possible incompressible motion. ∎

1.6 Euler's equations of motion; Lagrange's equations

🔥 High — 10-mark derivation
📜 Euler's equation $$\frac{D\mathbf q}{Dt}=\mathbf F-\frac1\rho\nabla p,\qquad\text{i.e.}\quad\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}+u\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}+v\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}+w\frac{\partial u}{\partial z}=X-\frac1\rho\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}$$ (and similarly for v, w).
✒️ Derivation
  1. Consider a volume V bounded by S moving with the liquid. Its mass \(\int_V\rho\,dV\) is constant.
  2. Forces on it: body force \(\int_V\rho\mathbf F\,dV\); pressure (normal thrust, inviscid liquid) \(-\int_Sp\,\mathbf n\,dS=-\int_V\nabla p\,dV\) (Gauss).
  3. Rate of change of momentum \(=\frac{D}{Dt}\int_V\rho\mathbf q\,dV=\int_V\rho\frac{D\mathbf q}{Dt}dV\) (mass of each element is conserved).
  4. Newton's second law: \(\int_V\big[\rho\frac{D\mathbf q}{Dt}-\rho\mathbf F+\nabla p\big]dV=0\) for every V.
  5. Hence \(\frac{D\mathbf q}{Dt}=\mathbf F-\frac1\rho\nabla p\). ∎
📜 Lamb's (vector) formWith \(\mathbf F=-\nabla\Omega\): $$\frac{\partial\mathbf q}{\partial t}-\mathbf q\times\boldsymbol\omega=-\nabla\Big(\tfrac12q^2+\Omega\Big)-\frac1\rho\nabla p .$$
✒️ DerivationUse the vector identity \((\mathbf q\cdot\nabla)\mathbf q=\nabla(\tfrac12q^2)-\mathbf q\times(\nabla\times\mathbf q)\) in \(\frac{\partial\mathbf q}{\partial t}+(\mathbf q\cdot\nabla)\mathbf q=-\nabla\Omega-\frac1\rho\nabla p\). ∎
📜 Lagrange's equations of motion $$\Big(\frac{\partial^2x}{\partial t^2}-X\Big)\frac{\partial x}{\partial a}+\Big(\frac{\partial^2y}{\partial t^2}-Y\Big)\frac{\partial y}{\partial a}+\Big(\frac{\partial^2z}{\partial t^2}-Z\Big)\frac{\partial z}{\partial a}=-\frac1\rho\frac{\partial p}{\partial a}$$ and two similar equations with b and c.
✒️ DerivationIn Lagrangian variables the acceleration is \((\ddot x,\ddot y,\ddot z)\), so Euler's equations read \(\ddot x-X=-\frac1\rho\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}\), etc. Multiply these three by \(\frac{\partial x}{\partial a},\frac{\partial y}{\partial a},\frac{\partial z}{\partial a}\) and add. By the chain rule \(\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}\frac{\partial x}{\partial a}+\frac{\partial p}{\partial y}\frac{\partial y}{\partial a}+\frac{\partial p}{\partial z}\frac{\partial z}{\partial a}=\frac{\partial p}{\partial a}\). This gives the first equation; b and c are similar. ∎

1.7 Bernoulli's equation

🔥 High — every paper
💡 ConceptJahan paani tez behta hai wahan pressure kam. Pressure-energy + kinetic energy + potential energy = constant.
📜 Theorem (a) — irrotational motionIf the motion is irrotational (\(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\)), the body force is conservative (\(\mathbf F=-\nabla\Omega\)) and ρ is a function of p only, then throughout the liquid $$\int\frac{dp}{\rho}+\frac12q^2+\Omega-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}=C(t).$$
✒️ Derivation
  1. In Lamb's form put ω = 0 and \(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\), so \(\frac{\partial\mathbf q}{\partial t}=-\nabla\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}\).
  2. Since ρ = ρ(p), \(\frac1\rho\nabla p=\nabla\int\frac{dp}\rho\).
  3. The equation becomes \(\nabla\Big(-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}+\frac12q^2+\Omega+\int\frac{dp}\rho\Big)=\mathbf 0\).
  4. So the bracket is independent of x, y, z: it is a function C(t) of time only. ∎
For steady irrotational motion C is an absolute constant; for an incompressible liquid under gravity \(\frac p\rho+\frac12q^2+gz=\) const everywhere.
📜 Theorem (b) — steady motion (rotational allowed)In steady motion with \(\mathbf F=-\nabla\Omega\) and ρ = ρ(p), \(\int\frac{dp}\rho+\frac12q^2+\Omega\) is constant along each streamline (and along each vortex line).
✒️ DerivationSteady: \(\frac{\partial\mathbf q}{\partial t}=0\), so Lamb's form gives \(\nabla H=\mathbf q\times\boldsymbol\omega\), \(H=\int\frac{dp}\rho+\frac12q^2+\Omega\). Take the dot product with a small element \(d\mathbf r\) along a streamline (parallel to q): \(dH=(\mathbf q\times\boldsymbol\omega)\cdot d\mathbf r=0\). So H is constant along the streamline (same for a vortex line, \(d\mathbf r\parallel\boldsymbol\omega\)). ∎
✍️ Solved example Q. Find the velocity of efflux from a small hole at depth h below the free surface of a large tank (Torricelli).
Apply steady Bernoulli along a streamline from the free surface (pressure \(p_0\), speed ≈ 0, height h) to the hole (pressure \(p_0\), speed v, height 0): \(\frac{p_0}\rho+0+gh=\frac{p_0}\rho+\frac12v^2+0\Rightarrow\boxed{v=\sqrt{2gh}}\). ∎

1.8 Equation of impulsive action

⭐ Medium
📖 DefinitionsAn impulsive force is a very large force acting for a very short time τ; impulse \(\mathbf I=\lim_{\tau\to0}\int_0^\tau\mathbf F\,dt\) (per unit mass). Impulsive pressure \(\varpi=\lim_{\tau\to0}\int_0^\tau p\,dt\).
📜 ResultIf the velocity changes suddenly from \(\mathbf q_1\) to \(\mathbf q_2\), $$\mathbf q_2-\mathbf q_1=\mathbf I-\frac1\rho\nabla\varpi .$$
✒️ Derivation
  1. Euler: \(\frac{D\mathbf q}{Dt}=\mathbf F-\frac1\rho\nabla p\). Integrate over the short interval (0, τ) following the particle; the particle hardly moves, and ρ is constant during the impulse.
  2. \(\int_0^\tau\frac{D\mathbf q}{Dt}dt=\mathbf q_2-\mathbf q_1\); \(\int_0^\tau\mathbf F\,dt\to\mathbf I\); \(\int_0^\tau\nabla p\,dt=\nabla\int_0^\tau p\,dt\to\nabla\varpi\).
  3. Hence \(\mathbf q_2-\mathbf q_1=\mathbf I-\frac1\rho\nabla\varpi\). ∎
Corollaries: if the liquid starts from rest (\(\mathbf q_1=0\)) and no impulsive body force acts (I = 0), then \(\mathbf q_2=-\frac1\rho\nabla\varpi\): the motion set up is irrotational with \(\phi=\frac{\varpi}{\rho}\) + const. Taking divergence for an incompressible liquid: \(\nabla^2\varpi=0\).

2.1 Stream function and complex potential

🔥 High
📖 Stream functionFor 2-D incompressible motion, continuity \(u_x+v_y=0\) makes \(v\,dx-u\,dy\) an exact differential \(d\psi\). The function ψ is the stream function: $$u=-\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial y},\qquad v=\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}.$$ The curves ψ = constant are the streamlines, and \(\psi_2-\psi_1\) is the volume flux (per unit thickness) between two streamlines.
✒️ Proofs
  1. Exactness: \(\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}=\frac{\partial(-u)}{\partial x}\) is exactly \(u_x+v_y=0\).
  2. On ψ = c: \(d\psi=v\,dx-u\,dy=0\Rightarrow\frac{dx}u=\frac{dy}v\), the streamline equation.
  3. Flux across a curve from A to B (unit normal \(\mathbf n\,ds=(dy,-dx)\) to the right of the direction of travel): \(\int(u\,dy-v\,dx)=-\int d\psi\), whose magnitude is \(|\psi_B-\psi_A|\). ∎
📜 Complex potentialIf the motion is also irrotational, \(u=-\phi_x=-\psi_y,\ v=-\phi_y=\psi_x\). These are the Cauchy–Riemann equations \(\phi_x=\psi_y,\ \phi_y=-\psi_x\), so \(w=\phi+i\psi\) is an analytic function of z = x + iy, and $$\frac{dw}{dz}=\phi_x+i\psi_x=-u+iv,\qquad q=\Big|\frac{dw}{dz}\Big| .$$ φ and ψ both satisfy Laplace's equation, and the families φ = const, ψ = const cut orthogonally.

✍️ Solved example Q. For \(w=z^2\), find φ, ψ, velocity and streamlines.
\(w=(x+iy)^2\Rightarrow\phi=x^2-y^2,\ \psi=2xy\). \(\frac{dw}{dz}=2z=-u+iv\Rightarrow u=-2x,\ v=2y\); speed \(q=2r\). Streamlines xy = const (rectangular hyperbolas) — flow in a right-angled corner. ∎

2.2 Sources, sinks and doublets

🔥 High
📖 DefinitionsA (2-D) source of strength m is a point from which liquid flows out radially and symmetrically, the volume emitted per unit time (per unit thickness) being 2πm. A sink is a negative source. A doublet is the limit of a source m and a sink −m at distance δs apart as m → ∞, δs → 0 with \(m\,\delta s=\mu\) finite; the line from sink to source is its axis.
✒️ Derivation — source
  1. By symmetry the velocity is radial, \(q_r(r)\). Volume through a circle of radius r per unit time: \(2\pi r\,q_r=2\pi m\Rightarrow q_r=\frac mr\).
  2. \(q_r=-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}=\frac mr\Rightarrow\phi=-m\log r\). Similarly \(\psi=-m\theta\) (from \(q_r=-\frac1r\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\theta}\)).
  3. \(w=\phi+i\psi=-m(\log r+i\theta)=-m\log z\). For a source at \(z_0\): \(w=-m\log(z-z_0)\). ∎
✒️ Derivation — doubletSource m at \(z_0+\delta s\,e^{i\alpha}\), sink m at \(z_0\): \(w=-m\log(z-z_0-\delta s\,e^{i\alpha})+m\log(z-z_0)=-m\log\Big(1-\frac{\delta s\,e^{i\alpha}}{z-z_0}\Big)\approx\frac{m\,\delta s\,e^{i\alpha}}{z-z_0}\). In the limit, \(w=\frac{\mu e^{i\alpha}}{z-z_0}\); for axis along the x-axis (α = 0) \(w=\frac{\mu}{z-z_0}\). With \(z_0=0\): \(\phi=\frac{\mu\cos\theta}{r},\ \psi=-\frac{\mu\sin\theta}{r}\); streamlines \(r=c\sin\theta\) are circles touching the x-axis at the origin. ∎
✍️ Solved example Q. A source m at (a, 0) and an equal sink at (−a, 0). Show that the streamlines are circles through the two points.
\(w=-m\log(z-a)+m\log(z+a)\Rightarrow\psi=-m(\theta_1-\theta_2)\), where \(\theta_1,\theta_2\) are the angles at P subtended from (a, 0) and (−a, 0). ψ = const ⇒ \(\theta_1-\theta_2\) = const, i.e. the angle APB subtended by AB at P is constant ⇒ P lies on a circle through A(a, 0) and B(−a, 0). ∎
💡 3-D source (Unit IV)Strength m: \(q_r=\frac m{r^2}\), \(\phi=\frac mr\), volume per unit time 4πm.

2.3 Images — with respect to a line, a circle and a sphere

🔥 High — circle case
📖 DefinitionIf a system S of sources, sinks, doublets in a liquid bounded by a rigid surface C can be replaced (in the region occupied by the liquid) by S together with another system S′ on the other side of C such that C becomes a streamline, then S′ is the image of S in C.
📜 (a) Image of a source in a straight line (plane wall)The image of a source m at (0, a) in the wall y = 0 is an equal source m at (0, −a).
✒️ Proof\(w=-m\log(z-ia)-m\log(z+ia)=-m\log(z^2+a^2)\). On y = 0, z = x is real, so \(z^2+a^2=x^2+a^2\gt0\) and \(\psi=-m\arg(x^2+a^2)=0\). Hence y = 0 is a streamline: no flow across the wall. ∎
📜 (b) Image of a source in a circleThe image of a source m at distance f (> a) from the centre of a circular cylinder of radius a consists of an equal source m at the inverse point (distance \(a^2/f\)) and an equal sink m at the centre.
✒️ Proof
  1. Take the centre at O and the source at z = f. Proposed system: \(w=-m\log(z-f)-m\log\big(z-\frac{a^2}{f}\big)+m\log z\).
  2. On the circle \(|z|=a\): \(z\bar z=a^2\), so \(z-\frac{a^2}{f}=z-\frac{z\bar z}{f}=\frac{z(f-\bar z)}{f}\).
  3. Hence on the circle \(w=-m\log(z-f)-m\log z-m\log(f-\bar z)+m\log f+m\log z=-m\log[(z-f)(f-\bar z)]+m\log f\).
  4. \((z-f)(f-\bar z)=-(z-f)\overline{(z-f)}=-|z-f|^2\), a negative real number; its argument is the constant π. So ψ = −mπ = constant on the circle.
  5. The circle is a streamline, so the system gives the motion outside the cylinder. ∎
📜 (c) Image of a doublet in a circleA doublet of strength μ at distance f from the centre (axis along OA) has as image a doublet of strength \(\mu a^2/f^2\) at the inverse point, its axis being the reflection of the original axis in OA. (Follows directly from the Milne-Thomson circle theorem, 3.1.)
📜 (d) Image of a source in a sphere (3-D)The image of a source of strength m at distance f from the centre of a sphere of radius a is a source \(\frac{ma}{f}\) at the inverse point (distance \(a^2/f\)) together with a uniform line sink of strength \(\frac ma\) per unit length extending from the centre to the inverse point.

2.4 Theorem of Blasius

🔥 High — full proof
📜 TheoremIn a steady 2-D irrotational motion with complex potential w, given by a fixed cylinder C, the force (X, Y) per unit length and the moment M about the origin exerted by the liquid on the cylinder are $$X-iY=\frac{i\rho}{2}\oint_C\Big(\frac{dw}{dz}\Big)^2dz,\qquad M=\operatorname{Re}\Big[-\frac\rho2\oint_Cz\Big(\frac{dw}{dz}\Big)^2dz\Big],$$ external forces being neglected.
✒️ Proof
  1. Take an element ds of the contour C (described counter-clockwise) at (x, y), with dz = dx + i dy. The pressure p acts along the inward normal; its components on the element are \(dX=-p\,dy,\ dY=p\,dx\).
  2. So \(dX-i\,dY=-p\,(dy+i\,dx)=-ip\,(dx-i\,dy)=-ip\,d\bar z\), and \(X-iY=-i\oint_Cp\,d\bar z\).
  3. Bernoulli (steady, no external force): \(p=C-\frac12\rho q^2\). Since \(\oint_C d\bar z=0\): \(X-iY=\frac{i\rho}{2}\oint_Cq^2\,d\bar z\).
  4. C is a streamline (ψ = const), so along C, \(dw=d\phi\) is real: \(\frac{dw}{dz}dz=\overline{\Big(\frac{dw}{dz}dz\Big)}=\frac{d\bar w}{d\bar z}d\bar z\).
  5. \(q^2=\frac{dw}{dz}\cdot\frac{d\bar w}{d\bar z}\), so \(q^2\,d\bar z=\frac{dw}{dz}\Big(\frac{d\bar w}{d\bar z}d\bar z\Big)=\frac{dw}{dz}\cdot\frac{dw}{dz}dz=\Big(\frac{dw}{dz}\Big)^2dz\).
  6. Therefore \(X-iY=\frac{i\rho}{2}\oint_C\big(\frac{dw}{dz}\big)^2dz\).
  7. Moment: \(dM=x\,dY-y\,dX=p(x\,dx+y\,dy)=\operatorname{Re}(p\,z\,d\bar z)\). With \(p=C-\frac12\rho q^2\) and \(\oint(x\,dx+y\,dy)=0\): \(M=\operatorname{Re}\big[-\frac\rho2\oint q^2z\,d\bar z\big]\), and by step 5, \(q^2z\,d\bar z=z\big(\frac{dw}{dz}\big)^2dz\). Hence \(M=\operatorname{Re}\big[-\frac\rho2\oint_Cz\big(\frac{dw}{dz}\big)^2dz\big]\). ∎
✍️ Application — Kutta–Joukowski lift Uniform stream U past a circular cylinder with circulation Γ: \(w=-U\big(z+\frac{a^2}{z}\big)+\frac{i\Gamma}{2\pi}\log z\).
\(\frac{dw}{dz}=-U+\frac{Ua^2}{z^2}+\frac{i\Gamma}{2\pi z}\). In \(\big(\frac{dw}{dz}\big)^2\) the coefficient of \(\frac1z\) is \(2(-U)\frac{i\Gamma}{2\pi}=-\frac{iU\Gamma}{\pi}\). By the residue theorem \(\oint\big(\frac{dw}{dz}\big)^2dz=2\pi i\cdot\big(-\frac{iU\Gamma}{\pi}\big)=2U\Gamma\).
\(X-iY=\frac{i\rho}{2}\cdot2U\Gamma=i\rho U\Gamma\Rightarrow X=0,\ Y=-\rho U\Gamma\). So there is no drag, and a force of magnitude \(\rho U\Gamma\) perpendicular to the stream (lift). ∎

2.5 Flow, circulation and Kelvin's circulation theorem

🔥 High — 10-mark proof
📖 DefinitionsThe flow along a curve from A to B is \(\int_A^B\mathbf q\cdot d\mathbf r=\int(u\,dx+v\,dy+w\,dz)\). If the curve is closed, the flow round it is the circulation \(\Gamma=\oint_C\mathbf q\cdot d\mathbf r\). By Stokes' theorem \(\Gamma=\int_S\boldsymbol\omega\cdot\mathbf n\,dS\). In irrotational motion, flow from A to B \(=\phi_A-\phi_B\).
📜 Kelvin's circulation theoremIn an inviscid liquid with conservative body forces (\(\mathbf F=-\nabla\Omega\)) and density a function of pressure only, the circulation round any closed circuit moving with the liquid is constant in time: \(\dfrac{D\Gamma}{Dt}=0\).
✒️ Proof
  1. \(\frac{D}{Dt}\oint_C\mathbf q\cdot d\mathbf r=\oint_C\frac{D\mathbf q}{Dt}\cdot d\mathbf r+\oint_C\mathbf q\cdot\frac{D}{Dt}(d\mathbf r)\).
  2. The circuit moves with the liquid, so \(\frac{D}{Dt}(d\mathbf r)=d\mathbf q\). Then \(\oint\mathbf q\cdot d\mathbf q=\oint d\big(\frac12q^2\big)=0\) (single-valued function round a closed curve).
  3. Euler's equation: \(\frac{D\mathbf q}{Dt}=-\nabla\Omega-\frac1\rho\nabla p=-\nabla\big(\Omega+\int\frac{dp}\rho\big)\). So \(\oint\frac{D\mathbf q}{Dt}\cdot d\mathbf r=-\oint d\big(\Omega+\int\frac{dp}\rho\big)=0\).
  4. Hence \(\frac{D\Gamma}{Dt}=0\). ∎
📜 Permanence of irrotational motionUnder the same conditions, if the motion is irrotational at any one instant, it remains irrotational for all time.
✒️ ProofIf the motion is irrotational at \(t_0\), then \(\Gamma=\int\boldsymbol\omega\cdot\mathbf n\,dS=0\) for every circuit. By Kelvin's theorem the circulation round each circuit moving with the liquid stays 0. So \(\int_S\boldsymbol\omega\cdot\mathbf n\,dS=0\) for every small surface at all later times, which forces ω = 0. ∎ (Hence liquid starting from rest under such forces moves irrotationally.)

2.6 Kinetic energy of liquid

⭐ Medium
📜 ResultFor irrotational motion of an incompressible liquid in region V with boundary S, $$T=\frac\rho2\int_Vq^2\,dV=\frac\rho2\int_S\phi\,\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}\,dS,$$ where n is the normal drawn out of the liquid. In 2-D (per unit length) along the boundary: \(T=-\frac\rho2\oint\phi\,d\psi\).
✒️ Derivation
  1. \(q^2=\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\phi\), and \(\nabla\cdot(\phi\nabla\phi)=\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\phi+\phi\nabla^2\phi=q^2\) since \(\nabla^2\phi=0\).
  2. By Gauss: \(\int_Vq^2dV=\int_V\nabla\cdot(\phi\nabla\phi)dV=\int_S\phi\,\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}dS\).
  3. Multiply by ρ/2. ∎
Infinite liquid: when the liquid extends to infinity and is at rest there, φ = O(1/r) (2-D: O(1/r) for a moving body without circulation; 3-D: O(1/r²)), so the integral over a large outer sphere/circle → 0 and only the body surface contributes.
📜 Kelvin's minimum energy theoremThe irrotational motion of a liquid in a simply connected region has less kinetic energy than any other motion with the same normal velocities at the boundary.

2.7 Euler's momentum theorem and D'Alembert's paradox

🔥 High — paradox
📜 Euler's momentum theoremFor liquid inside a fixed closed surface S (volume V): $$\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\int_V\rho\mathbf q\,dV=\int_V\rho\mathbf F\,dV-\int_Sp\,\mathbf n\,dS-\int_S\rho\,\mathbf q\,(\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf n)\,dS .$$ In steady motion: the resultant of the body forces and the pressure thrusts on the liquid inside S equals the rate at which momentum flows out across S.
✒️ DerivationMomentum inside S changes because of (i) forces on the liquid inside — body force \(\int\rho\mathbf F\,dV\) and surface pressure \(-\int p\,\mathbf n\,dS\); and (ii) momentum carried across S by the liquid: through dS the volume \(\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf n\,dS\) leaves per unit time, carrying momentum \(\rho\mathbf q(\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf n)dS\). Equating gives the result. ∎
📜 D'Alembert's paradoxA body moving with uniform velocity through an infinite ideal liquid, in irrotational motion without circulation, experiences no resultant force (no drag).
✒️ Demonstration for a circular cylinder
  1. Uniform stream U past a fixed cylinder (equivalent to the cylinder moving with −U): \(w=-U\big(z+\frac{a^2}{z}\big)\); on r = a the speed is \(q=2U\sin\theta\) (see 3.1).
  2. Bernoulli: \(p+\frac12\rho q^2=p_\infty+\frac12\rho U^2\Rightarrow p=p_\infty+\frac12\rho U^2(1-4\sin^2\theta)\).
  3. Drag \(X=-\int_0^{2\pi}p\cos\theta\,a\,d\theta\) and lift \(Y=-\int_0^{2\pi}p\sin\theta\,a\,d\theta\). Since \(\int_0^{2\pi}\cos\theta\,d\theta=\int_0^{2\pi}\sin^2\theta\cos\theta\,d\theta=0\) and similarly with sin θ, X = Y = 0.
  4. Pressure is symmetric fore-and-aft (θ ↔ π − θ) and above-below, so the thrusts cancel. ∎ (Also from Blasius with Γ = 0.) The "paradox": real bodies feel drag — because real liquids are viscous.

3.1 Milne-Thomson circle theorem; stream past a circular cylinder

🔥 High — most important theorem
📜 TheoremLet f(z) be the complex potential of a 2-D irrotational flow having no singularities within \(|z|\le a\). If a circular cylinder \(|z|=a\) is introduced into the flow, the complex potential becomes $$w=f(z)+\bar f\Big(\frac{a^2}{z}\Big),$$ where \(\bar f(z)=\overline{f(\bar z)}\).
✒️ Proof
  1. Boundary condition. On \(|z|=a\), \(\bar z=\frac{a^2}{z}\). So \(\bar f\big(\frac{a^2}{z}\big)=\bar f(\bar z)=\overline{f(z)}\), and \(w=f(z)+\overline{f(z)}=2\operatorname{Re}f(z)\), which is real. Hence ψ = 0 on the circle: the circle is a streamline.
  2. No new singularities outside. If z is outside the circle (|z| > a), then \(\big|\frac{a^2}{z}\big|\lt a\). All singularities of f lie outside the circle, so those of \(\bar f(a^2/z)\) lie inside it. Thus outside the cylinder w has exactly the singularities of f.
  3. Far field. As \(|z|\to\infty\), \(\frac{a^2}{z}\to0\), a regular point of \(\bar f\), so the added term tends to a constant and the original flow is recovered at large distances.
  4. Hence w is the required complex potential. ∎
✍️ Solved example — uniform stream past a fixed circular cylinder
  1. \(f(z)=-Uz\Rightarrow\bar f(z)=-Uz\Rightarrow\bar f(a^2/z)=-\frac{Ua^2}{z}\).
  2. \(\boxed{w=-U\Big(z+\frac{a^2}{z}\Big)}\), so \(\phi=-U\big(r+\frac{a^2}{r}\big)\cos\theta,\ \psi=-U\big(r-\frac{a^2}{r}\big)\sin\theta\).
  3. On r = a: \(q_r=0\), \(q_\theta=-\frac1r\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\theta}=-2U\sin\theta\), so speed \(q=2U|\sin\theta|\).
  4. Stagnation points θ = 0, π; maximum speed 2U at θ = ±π/2.
  5. Pressure: \(p=p_\infty+\frac12\rho U^2(1-4\sin^2\theta)\).
✍️ Solved example — image of a source by the circle theoremSource m at z = f (f > a): \(f(z)=-m\log(z-f)\). \(\bar f(a^2/z)=-m\log\big(\frac{a^2}{z}-f\big)=-m\log\big(z-\frac{a^2}{f}\big)+m\log z+\text{const}\). So the image is a source m at \(a^2/f\) and a sink m at the centre — same as 2.3(b). ∎

3.2 Circular cylinder moving in liquid at rest

🔥 High
📜 ResultA circular cylinder of radius a moving with velocity U (along x) in an infinite liquid at rest at infinity: $$w=\frac{Ua^2}{z},\quad\phi=\frac{Ua^2}{r}\cos\theta,\quad T=\frac12\pi\rho a^2U^2=\frac12M'U^2,$$ where \(M'=\pi\rho a^2\) is the mass of liquid displaced per unit length. Equation of motion under external force F: \((M+M')\dfrac{dU}{dt}=F\).
✒️ Derivation
  1. φ must satisfy Laplace's equation, vanish at infinity, and give the right normal velocity on r = a. Try \(\phi=\frac{A}{r}\cos\theta\).
  2. On r = a the liquid's normal velocity equals the cylinder's: \(-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}=U\cos\theta\Rightarrow\frac{A}{a^2}=U\Rightarrow A=Ua^2\). So \(\phi=\frac{Ua^2}{r}\cos\theta\), \(w=\frac{Ua^2}{z}\), \(\psi=-\frac{Ua^2}{r}\sin\theta\).
  3. KE: \(T=-\frac\rho2\oint\phi\,d\psi\). On r = a: \(\phi=Ua\cos\theta\), \(d\psi=-Ua\cos\theta\,d\theta\). \(T=-\frac\rho2\int_0^{2\pi}Ua\cos\theta(-Ua\cos\theta)d\theta=\frac\rho2U^2a^2\pi\).
  4. Energy equation: rate of work by F = rate of increase of total KE: \(FU=\frac{d}{dt}\big[\frac12(M+M')U^2\big]=(M+M')U\frac{dU}{dt}\). ∎
✍️ Solved example Q. A circular cylinder of density σ is released from rest in a liquid of density ρ (gravity along the axis of motion, the cylinder's axis horizontal). Find its initial acceleration.
Per unit length, mass \(M=\pi a^2\sigma\), added mass \(M'=\pi a^2\rho\), net force \(F=(M-M')g=\pi a^2(\sigma-\rho)g\). \((\sigma+\rho)\pi a^2\frac{dU}{dt}=(\sigma-\rho)\pi a^2g\Rightarrow\boxed{\frac{dU}{dt}=\frac{\sigma-\rho}{\sigma+\rho}\,g}\). ∎

3.3 Elliptic cylinder

⭐ Medium
📖 Elliptic coordinates\(z=c\cosh\zeta\), \(\zeta=\xi+i\eta\): \(x=c\cosh\xi\cos\eta,\ y=c\sinh\xi\sin\eta\). The curves ξ = const are confocal ellipses with foci (±c, 0). The cylinder is ξ = α with semi-axes \(a=c\cosh\alpha,\ b=c\sinh\alpha\); so \(c^2=a^2-b^2\) and \(e^\alpha=\frac{a+b}{c}\).
📜 (a) Elliptic cylinder moving along its major axis with velocity U $$w=Ub\sqrt{\frac{a+b}{a-b}}\;e^{-\zeta},\qquad T=\frac12\pi\rho b^2U^2 .$$
✒️ Derivation
  1. Boundary condition for a body moving with velocity U along x: across an element of the boundary the normal flux of liquid equals that of the body, which gives \(\psi=-Uy+\text{const}\) on the boundary (with \(u=-\psi_y\), \(v=\psi_x\)).
  2. The motion must die out at infinity, where \(z\approx\frac c2e^\zeta\). Try \(w=Ae^{-\zeta}\) (A real): \(\psi=-Ae^{-\xi}\sin\eta\).
  3. On ξ = α: \(\psi=-Ae^{-\alpha}\sin\eta\), while \(-Uy=-Uc\sinh\alpha\sin\eta=-Ub\sin\eta\). Matching: \(Ae^{-\alpha}=Ub\Rightarrow A=Ube^{\alpha}=Ub\frac{a+b}{c}=Ub\sqrt{\frac{a+b}{a-b}}\).
  4. KE \(=-\frac\rho2\oint\phi\,d\psi\). On ξ = α: \(\phi=Ae^{-\alpha}\cos\eta=Ub\cos\eta\), \(\psi=-Ub\sin\eta\), \(d\psi=-Ub\cos\eta\,d\eta\). \(T=-\frac\rho2\int_0^{2\pi}Ub\cos\eta(-Ub\cos\eta)\,d\eta=\frac12\pi\rho b^2U^2\). ∎
Moving along the minor axis (velocity V along y): similarly \(T=\frac12\pi\rho a^2V^2\).
📜 (b) Uniform stream U (along major axis) past a fixed elliptic cylinder $$w=-U(a+b)\cosh(\zeta-\alpha).$$
✒️ Verification
  1. On ξ = α: \(\cosh(\zeta-\alpha)=\cosh(i\eta)=\cos\eta\), real ⇒ ψ = 0: the ellipse is a streamline.
  2. At infinity \(\cosh(\zeta-\alpha)\approx\frac12e^{\zeta-\alpha}\) and \(z\approx\frac c2e^\zeta\), so \(w\approx-U(a+b)e^{-\alpha}\frac zc=-Uz\) (since \((a+b)e^{-\alpha}=c\)). The uniform stream is recovered.
  3. w is regular outside the ellipse. ∎
🧠 TrickAdded mass me doosri semi-axis aati hai: major axis ke along chalo to \(\pi\rho b^2\), minor ke along to \(\pi\rho a^2\). Circle (a = b) me \(\pi\rho a^2\) ✓.

3.4 Initial motion between two coaxial circular cylinders

⭐ Medium
📜 ResultA circular cylinder of radius a is suddenly moved with velocity U inside a fixed coaxial cylinder of radius b, the space between being filled with liquid. Then $$\phi=\frac{Ua^2}{b^2-a^2}\Big(r+\frac{b^2}{r}\Big)\cos\theta,\qquad T=\frac12\pi\rho a^2U^2\,\frac{b^2+a^2}{b^2-a^2}.$$
✒️ Derivation
  1. Liquid starting from rest moves irrotationally (1.8). Try \(\phi=\big(Ar+\frac Br\big)\cos\theta\), which satisfies Laplace's equation.
  2. Outer cylinder fixed: \(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}=0\) at r = b ⇒ \(A-\frac{B}{b^2}=0\).
  3. Inner cylinder: \(-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}=U\cos\theta\) at r = a ⇒ \(-\big(A-\frac{B}{a^2}\big)=U\).
  4. Solving: \(B=\frac{Ua^2b^2}{b^2-a^2},\ A=\frac{Ua^2}{b^2-a^2}\).
  5. KE: only the inner surface contributes (∂φ/∂n = 0 on the outer). On r = a, with n out of the liquid (into the inner cylinder), \(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}=-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}=U\cos\theta\), and \(\phi=\big(Aa+\frac Ba\big)\cos\theta=\frac{Ua(a^2+b^2)}{b^2-a^2}\cos\theta\).
  6. \(T=\frac\rho2\int_0^{2\pi}\frac{Ua(a^2+b^2)}{b^2-a^2}\cos\theta\cdot U\cos\theta\cdot a\,d\theta=\frac12\pi\rho a^2U^2\frac{b^2+a^2}{b^2-a^2}\). ∎
Check: b → ∞ gives \(\frac12\pi\rho a^2U^2\), the single cylinder.

4.1 Irrotational motion in three dimensions (axial symmetry)

⭐ Medium
📜 ResultFor motion symmetric about an axis, in spherical polars (r, θ), Laplace's equation is $$\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\Big(r^2\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}\Big)+\frac1{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\Big(\sin\theta\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\theta}\Big)=0,$$ with solutions \(\phi=\big(Ar^n+\frac{B}{r^{n+1}}\big)P_n(\cos\theta)\), \(P_n\) = Legendre polynomial.
✒️ Check for n = 1Try \(\phi=R(r)\cos\theta\). The θ-part gives \(\frac1{\sin\theta}\frac{d}{d\theta}(-\sin^2\theta)=-2\cos\theta\). So \((r^2R')'-2R=0\). Putting \(R=r^k\): \(k(k+1)=2\Rightarrow k=1\) or \(k=-2\). Hence \(\phi=\big(Ar+\frac{B}{r^2}\big)\cos\theta\) — used in all sphere problems. ∎

4.2 Sphere moving in liquid at rest at infinity

🔥 High
📜 Result $$\phi=\frac{Ua^3}{2r^2}\cos\theta,\qquad T=\frac13\pi\rho a^3U^2=\frac12\Big(\frac{M'}{2}\Big)U^2,\quad M'=\frac43\pi a^3\rho .$$ The added (virtual) mass of a sphere is half the mass of liquid displaced.
✒️ Derivation
  1. The liquid is at rest at infinity, so take \(\phi=\frac{B}{r^2}\cos\theta\) (the Ar term would grow at infinity).
  2. On r = a the normal velocity of the liquid equals that of the sphere: \(-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}=U\cos\theta\Rightarrow\frac{2B}{a^3}=U\Rightarrow B=\frac{Ua^3}{2}\).
  3. KE: \(T=\frac\rho2\int_S\phi\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}dS\), n into the sphere, \(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}=-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}=U\cos\theta\); on r = a, \(\phi=\frac{Ua}{2}\cos\theta\); \(dS=2\pi a^2\sin\theta\,d\theta\).
  4. \(T=\frac\rho2\int_0^\pi\frac{Ua}{2}\cos\theta\cdot U\cos\theta\cdot2\pi a^2\sin\theta\,d\theta=\frac{\pi\rho a^3U^2}{2}\cdot\frac23=\frac13\pi\rho a^3U^2\). ∎

4.3 Liquid streaming past a fixed sphere

🔥 High
📜 Result $$\phi=-U\Big(r+\frac{a^3}{2r^2}\Big)\cos\theta,\qquad q\big|_{r=a}=\frac32U\sin\theta,\qquad p=p_\infty+\frac12\rho U^2\Big(1-\frac94\sin^2\theta\Big).$$
✒️ Derivation
  1. Far away the stream is uniform: \(\phi\to-Ux=-Ur\cos\theta\). Take \(\phi=\big(-Ur+\frac{B}{r^2}\big)\cos\theta\).
  2. Fixed sphere: \(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}=0\) at r = a ⇒ \(-U-\frac{2B}{a^3}=0\Rightarrow B=-\frac{Ua^3}{2}\).
  3. On r = a: \(q_r=0\), \(q_\theta=-\frac1r\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\theta}=-U\big(1+\frac12\big)\sin\theta\), so \(q=\frac32U\sin\theta\) (max \(\frac32U\) at θ = π/2; stagnation at θ = 0, π).
  4. Bernoulli: \(p+\frac12\rho q^2=p_\infty+\frac12\rho U^2\) gives the pressure. ∎
(This is the sphere-moving solution with a uniform stream −U superposed, which brings the sphere to rest.)
🧠 CompareCylinder: max speed 2U, added mass = displaced mass. Sphere: max speed 1.5U, added mass = ½ displaced mass.

4.4 Equation of motion of a sphere; sphere under gravity

🔥 High — numerical
📜 ResultA sphere of mass M moving in a straight line through liquid at rest at infinity under an external force F obeys $$\Big(M+\frac{M'}{2}\Big)\frac{dU}{dt}=F .$$ Under gravity (sphere density σ, liquid density ρ, volume V): \(\big(\sigma+\frac\rho2\big)V\frac{dU}{dt}=(\sigma-\rho)Vg\), i.e. $$\frac{dU}{dt}=\frac{2(\sigma-\rho)}{2\sigma+\rho}\,g .$$
✒️ Derivation
  1. Total KE = KE of sphere + KE of liquid \(=\frac12MU^2+\frac12\cdot\frac{M'}{2}U^2\) (4.2).
  2. Rate of work of external forces = rate of increase of KE: \(FU=\big(M+\frac{M'}2\big)U\frac{dU}{dt}\). Divide by U.
  3. Under gravity the external force is weight − buoyancy \(=(\sigma-\rho)Vg\) (buoyancy from the hydrostatic part of the pressure), and \(M=\sigma V,\ M'=\rho V\). ∎
✍️ Solved example Q. A hollow sphere of negligible mass (σ ≈ 0) is released from rest under water. Find its initial acceleration.
\(\frac{dU}{dt}=\frac{2(0-\rho)}{0+\rho}g=-2g\): it starts moving upwards with acceleration 2g. ∎

4.5 Concentric spheres

⭐ Medium
📜 ResultA sphere of radius a moves with velocity U inside a fixed concentric sphere of radius b, the space between filled with liquid. Then $$\phi=\frac{Ua^3}{b^3-a^3}\Big(r+\frac{b^3}{2r^2}\Big)\cos\theta,\qquad T=\frac13\pi\rho a^3U^2\,\frac{b^3+2a^3}{b^3-a^3}.$$
✒️ Derivation
  1. Take \(\phi=\big(Ar+\frac{B}{r^2}\big)\cos\theta\); \(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}=\big(A-\frac{2B}{r^3}\big)\cos\theta\).
  2. Outer sphere fixed: \(A-\frac{2B}{b^3}=0\). Inner sphere: \(-\big(A-\frac{2B}{a^3}\big)=U\).
  3. Solving: \(B=\frac{Ua^3b^3}{2(b^3-a^3)},\ A=\frac{Ua^3}{b^3-a^3}\).
  4. On r = a: \(\phi=\big(Aa+\frac B{a^2}\big)\cos\theta=\frac{Ua(2a^3+b^3)}{2(b^3-a^3)}\cos\theta\), \(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial n}=U\cos\theta\).
  5. \(T=\frac\rho2\int_0^\pi\frac{Ua(2a^3+b^3)}{2(b^3-a^3)}\cos\theta\cdot U\cos\theta\cdot2\pi a^2\sin\theta\,d\theta=\frac13\pi\rho a^3U^2\frac{b^3+2a^3}{b^3-a^3}\). ∎
Check: b → ∞ gives \(\frac13\pi\rho a^3U^2\).

4.6 Stokes's stream function

🔥 High
📖 DefinitionFor incompressible motion symmetric about an axis, in spherical polars (r, θ), Stokes's stream function ψ is defined by $$q_r=\frac{1}{r^2\sin\theta}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\theta},\qquad q_\theta=-\frac{1}{r\sin\theta}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial r}.$$ The surfaces ψ = const are stream surfaces, and \(2\pi(\psi_2-\psi_1)\) is the volume flux between two of them. (Some books use the opposite overall sign.)
✒️ Existence
  1. Continuity for axisymmetric flow: \(\frac{\partial}{\partial r}(r^2\sin\theta\,q_r)+\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}(r\sin\theta\,q_\theta)=0\).
  2. This is the condition for \(r^2\sin\theta\,q_r\,d\theta-r\sin\theta\,q_\theta\,dr\) to be an exact differential \(d\psi\). Then \(\psi_\theta=r^2\sin\theta\,q_r\) and \(\psi_r=-r\sin\theta\,q_\theta\), which is the definition. ∎
  3. Flux through the cap of a sphere of radius r from θ = 0 to θ: \(\int_0^\theta q_r\,2\pi r^2\sin\theta\,d\theta=2\pi[\psi(\theta)-\psi(0)]\).
FlowStokes ψ
Uniform stream U along the axis\(\frac12Ur^2\sin^2\theta\)
3-D source m at origin\(-m\cos\theta\)
Stream U past a fixed sphere\(\frac12U\sin^2\theta\Big(r^2-\frac{a^3}{r}\Big)\)
✍️ Verification — stream past a sphere \(\psi_\theta=U\sin\theta\cos\theta\big(r^2-\frac{a^3}r\big)\Rightarrow q_r=U\cos\theta\big(1-\frac{a^3}{r^3}\big)\); \(\psi_r=\frac12U\sin^2\theta\big(2r+\frac{a^3}{r^2}\big)\Rightarrow q_\theta=-U\sin\theta\big(1+\frac{a^3}{2r^3}\big)\). These agree with φ of 4.3. On r = a, ψ = 0, so the sphere is a stream surface. ∎
