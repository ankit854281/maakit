Section A — Short answer

Attempt all questions. 5 × 3 = 15 marks

Q1. Distinguish between the Lagrangian and Eulerian methods of describing fluid motion. 3

Solution
Solution Lagrangian: an individual particle is followed; its position \(\mathbf r=\mathbf r(a,b,c,t)\) is studied, (a, b, c) being its initial position. Independent variables: a, b, c, t.
Eulerian: attention is fixed on points of space; velocity \(\mathbf q(x,y,z,t)\), pressure, density are studied there as functions of time. Independent variables: x, y, z, t.
The Lagrangian method gives the history of each particle; the Eulerian gives the flow field and is mathematically simpler. They are connected by \(\frac{D}{Dt}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\mathbf q\cdot\nabla\).

Q2. Find the streamlines for the flow \(u=-\omega y,\ v=\omega x\) (ω constant). 3

Solution
Solution \(\frac{dx}{-\omega y}=\frac{dy}{\omega x}\Rightarrow x\,dx+y\,dy=0\Rightarrow\boxed{x^2+y^2=c}\). The streamlines are concentric circles about the origin (rigid rotation). Vorticity \(\zeta=v_x-u_y=2\omega\ne0\): the motion is rotational.

Q3. Show that \(\phi=x^2-y^2\) represents a possible irrotational motion of an incompressible liquid and find the stream function. 3

Solution
Solution \(\nabla^2\phi=2-2=0\): continuity holds, so the motion is possible; it is irrotational because a velocity potential exists. \(w=\phi+i\psi\) with \(\phi=\operatorname{Re}(z^2)\), so \(w=z^2\) and \(\boxed{\psi=2xy}\). (Check: \(u=-\phi_x=-2x=-\psi_y\), \(v=-\phi_y=2y=\psi_x\).)

Q4. Write the complex potential of a source, a sink and a doublet. What is the image of a source in a plane wall? 3

Solution
Solution Source m at \(z_0\): \(w=-m\log(z-z_0)\). Sink m: \(w=m\log(z-z_0)\). Doublet μ (axis along x) at \(z_0\): \(w=\frac{\mu}{z-z_0}\).
The image of a source in a plane wall is an equal source at the mirror-image point on the other side of the wall.

Q5. State D'Alembert's paradox. 3

Solution
Solution A body moving with uniform velocity through an infinite ideal (inviscid, incompressible) liquid, the motion being irrotational and without circulation, experiences no resultant force (zero drag). It is a paradox because real bodies do feel drag; the discrepancy arises from neglecting viscosity.

Section B — Medium answer

Attempt any four. 4 × 7½ = 30 marks

Q6. Show that \(\frac{x^2}{a^2k^2t^4}+kt^2\left(\frac{y^2}{b^2}+\frac{z^2}{c^2}\right)=1\) is a possible form of the boundary surface of a liquid. 7½

Solution
Solution A surface F = 0 is a boundary surface if \(\frac{DF}{Dt}=0\) for some velocity satisfying continuity. Let \(F=\frac{x^2}{a^2k^2t^4}+kt^2\big(\frac{y^2}{b^2}+\frac{z^2}{c^2}\big)-1\) and try \(u=\frac{2x}t,\ v=-\frac yt,\ w=-\frac zt\).
\(\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}=-\frac{4x^2}{a^2k^2t^5}+2kt\big(\frac{y^2}{b^2}+\frac{z^2}{c^2}\big)\); \(uF_x=\frac{4x^2}{a^2k^2t^5}\); \(vF_y=-\frac{2kty^2}{b^2}\); \(wF_z=-\frac{2ktz^2}{c^2}\).
Sum = 0, so DF/Dt = 0. Continuity: \(u_x+v_y+w_z=\frac2t-\frac1t-\frac1t=0\). Hence F = 0 is a possible boundary surface. ∎

Q7. Derive Bernoulli's equation for steady motion of an ideal fluid under conservative forces. 7½

Solution
Solution
  1. Euler: \(\frac{\partial\mathbf q}{\partial t}+(\mathbf q\cdot\nabla)\mathbf q=-\nabla\Omega-\frac1\rho\nabla p\), with \(\mathbf F=-\nabla\Omega\).
  2. Identity: \((\mathbf q\cdot\nabla)\mathbf q=\nabla(\frac12q^2)-\mathbf q\times\boldsymbol\omega\). If ρ = ρ(p), \(\frac1\rho\nabla p=\nabla\int\frac{dp}\rho\).
  3. Steady: \(\frac{\partial\mathbf q}{\partial t}=0\). Hence \(\nabla H=\mathbf q\times\boldsymbol\omega\), \(H=\int\frac{dp}\rho+\frac12q^2+\Omega\).
  4. Take the dot product with \(d\mathbf r\) along a streamline (\(d\mathbf r\parallel\mathbf q\)): \(dH=(\mathbf q\times\boldsymbol\omega)\cdot d\mathbf r=0\).
  5. So \(\int\frac{dp}\rho+\frac12q^2+\Omega=\) constant along each streamline. For an incompressible liquid under gravity: \(\boxed{\frac p\rho+\frac12q^2+gz=\text{const}}\). If the motion is also irrotational, the constant is the same everywhere. ∎

Q8. A source of strength m is at distance f from the centre of a fixed circular cylinder of radius a (f > a). Find the image system and verify that the cylinder is a streamline. 7½

Solution
Solution By the Milne-Thomson circle theorem with \(f(z)=-m\log(z-f)\): \(\bar f(a^2/z)=-m\log\big(\frac{a^2}z-f\big)=-m\log\big(z-\frac{a^2}f\big)+m\log z-m\log(-f)\).
So (dropping a constant) \(w=-m\log(z-f)-m\log\big(z-\frac{a^2}{f}\big)+m\log z\): image = source m at the inverse point \(a^2/f\) + sink m at the centre.
Verification: on |z| = a, \(z-\frac{a^2}f=\frac{z(f-\bar z)}{f}\), so \(w=-m\log[(z-f)(f-\bar z)]+m\log f=-m\log(-|z-f|^2)+m\log f\). The argument of \(-|z-f|^2\) is π (constant), so ψ is constant on the circle: it is a streamline. ∎

Q9. State and prove Kelvin's circulation theorem. 7½

Solution
Solution Statement. In an inviscid fluid moving under conservative body forces, with density a function of pressure only, the circulation round any closed circuit moving with the fluid remains constant.
Proof. \(\Gamma=\oint_C\mathbf q\cdot d\mathbf r\). \(\frac{D\Gamma}{Dt}=\oint\frac{D\mathbf q}{Dt}\cdot d\mathbf r+\oint\mathbf q\cdot\frac{D}{Dt}d\mathbf r\). Since \(\frac{D}{Dt}d\mathbf r=d\mathbf q\), the second integral is \(\oint d(\frac12q^2)=0\). By Euler, \(\frac{D\mathbf q}{Dt}=-\nabla(\Omega+\int\frac{dp}\rho)\), so the first integral is \(-\oint d(\Omega+\int\frac{dp}\rho)=0\). Hence \(\frac{D\Gamma}{Dt}=0\). ∎
Corollary. Motion once irrotational remains irrotational (permanence of irrotational motion).

Q10. A circular cylinder of radius a moves with velocity U in an infinite liquid at rest at infinity. Find the velocity potential and the kinetic energy of the liquid. 7½

Solution
Solution Take \(\phi=\frac Ar\cos\theta\) (Laplace's equation, vanishes at ∞). On r = a, \(-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}=U\cos\theta\Rightarrow A=Ua^2\). So \(\boxed{\phi=\frac{Ua^2}{r}\cos\theta,\ w=\frac{Ua^2}{z}}\), \(\psi=-\frac{Ua^2}{r}\sin\theta\).
KE per unit length \(T=-\frac\rho2\oint\phi\,d\psi\). On r = a: \(\phi=Ua\cos\theta\), \(d\psi=-Ua\cos\theta\,d\theta\): \(T=\frac\rho2U^2a^2\int_0^{2\pi}\cos^2\theta\,d\theta=\boxed{\frac12\pi\rho a^2U^2}\) = ½ (mass of liquid displaced) U².

Q11. A sphere of density σ is released from rest in a liquid of density ρ. Find its initial acceleration. 7½

Solution
Solution Liquid motion: \(\phi=\frac{Ua^3}{2r^2}\cos\theta\); KE of liquid \(=\frac13\pi\rho a^3U^2=\frac12\cdot\frac{M'}2U^2\), \(M'=\frac43\pi a^3\rho\).
Total KE \(=\frac12\big(M+\frac{M'}{2}\big)U^2\), \(M=\frac43\pi a^3\sigma\). External force (weight − buoyancy) \(=\frac43\pi a^3(\sigma-\rho)g\).
Energy: \(\big(M+\frac{M'}2\big)U\frac{dU}{dt}=\frac43\pi a^3(\sigma-\rho)gU\Rightarrow\big(\sigma+\frac\rho2\big)\frac{dU}{dt}=(\sigma-\rho)g\).
\(\boxed{\frac{dU}{dt}=\frac{2(\sigma-\rho)}{2\sigma+\rho}\,g}\) (downward if σ > ρ). For a very light sphere (σ → 0), upward acceleration 2g.

Attempt any three. 3 × 10 = 30 marks

Q12. Derive the equation of continuity in the Eulerian form. Deduce its form for an incompressible fluid and for irrotational motion of an incompressible fluid. 10

Solution
Solution
  1. Let S be a closed surface fixed in space, enclosing volume V; n = outward unit normal. Mass inside \(=\int_V\rho\,dV\).
  2. Rate of increase of mass inside \(=\int_V\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}dV\).
  3. Rate at which mass flows out across S \(=\int_S\rho\mathbf q\cdot\mathbf n\,dS=\int_V\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf q)dV\) (Gauss divergence theorem).
  4. No sources/sinks inside: \(\int_V\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}dV=-\int_V\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf q)dV\), i.e. \(\int_V\big[\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf q)\big]dV=0\).
  5. V is arbitrary and the integrand continuous, so \(\boxed{\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf q)=0}\), or in Cartesian form \(\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\frac{\partial(\rho u)}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial(\rho v)}{\partial y}+\frac{\partial(\rho w)}{\partial z}=0\).
  6. Expanding: \(\frac{D\rho}{Dt}+\rho\nabla\cdot\mathbf q=0\).
  7. Incompressible (\(\frac{D\rho}{Dt}=0\)): \(\nabla\cdot\mathbf q=0\), i.e. \(u_x+v_y+w_z=0\).
  8. Irrotational and incompressible (\(\mathbf q=-\nabla\phi\)): \(\nabla\cdot(-\nabla\phi)=0\Rightarrow\boxed{\nabla^2\phi=0}\) (Laplace's equation). ∎

Q13. State and prove the theorem of Blasius. Use it to find the force on a circular cylinder placed in a uniform stream U with circulation Γ. 10

Solution
Solution Statement. In steady 2-D irrotational flow with complex potential w past a fixed cylinder C (no external forces), the thrust is given by \(X-iY=\frac{i\rho}2\oint_C\big(\frac{dw}{dz}\big)^2dz\).
Proof.
  1. On an element ds of C (counter-clockwise), pressure acts along the inward normal: \(dX=-p\,dy,\ dY=p\,dx\). So \(dX-i\,dY=-ip(dx-i\,dy)=-ip\,d\bar z\).
  2. Bernoulli: \(p=C-\frac12\rho q^2\); \(\oint d\bar z=0\). So \(X-iY=\frac{i\rho}2\oint q^2\,d\bar z\).
  3. C is a streamline, so \(dw=d\phi\) is real on C: \(\frac{dw}{dz}dz=\frac{d\bar w}{d\bar z}d\bar z\).
  4. \(q^2d\bar z=\frac{dw}{dz}\frac{d\bar w}{d\bar z}d\bar z=\big(\frac{dw}{dz}\big)^2dz\). Hence the result. ∎
Application. \(w=-U\big(z+\frac{a^2}z\big)+\frac{i\Gamma}{2\pi}\log z\); \(\frac{dw}{dz}=-U+\frac{Ua^2}{z^2}+\frac{i\Gamma}{2\pi z}\). Coefficient of \(\frac1z\) in \(\big(\frac{dw}{dz}\big)^2\): \(2(-U)\frac{i\Gamma}{2\pi}=-\frac{iU\Gamma}\pi\) (other terms have no 1/z). By the residue theorem \(\oint\big(\frac{dw}{dz}\big)^2dz=2\pi i\big(-\frac{iU\Gamma}{\pi}\big)=2U\Gamma\).
\(X-iY=\frac{i\rho}{2}\cdot2U\Gamma=i\rho U\Gamma\Rightarrow\boxed{X=0,\ |Y|=\rho U\Gamma}\): no drag; a lift ρUΓ per unit length perpendicular to the stream (Kutta–Joukowski).

Q14. State and prove the Milne-Thomson circle theorem. Hence find the complex potential for a uniform stream U past a fixed circular cylinder of radius a, the stagnation points and the pressure on the cylinder. 10

Solution
Solution Statement. If f(z) is the complex potential of a flow with no singularities in |z| ≤ a, then on introducing the cylinder |z| = a the complex potential becomes \(w=f(z)+\bar f(a^2/z)\).
Proof.
  1. On |z| = a, \(\bar z=a^2/z\), so \(\bar f(a^2/z)=\overline{f(z)}\) and \(w=f(z)+\overline{f(z)}\) is real: ψ = 0, the circle is a streamline.
  2. For |z| > a, \(|a^2/z|\lt a\); singularities of \(\bar f(a^2/z)\) are inside the circle, so no new singularities appear in the liquid.
  3. As z → ∞, \(\bar f(a^2/z)\to\bar f(0)\) (constant), so the original flow is recovered far away. ∎
Application. \(f(z)=-Uz\Rightarrow w=-U\big(z+\frac{a^2}z\big)\). \(\frac{dw}{dz}=-U\big(1-\frac{a^2}{z^2}\big)\). On \(z=ae^{i\theta}\): \(q=U|1-e^{-2i\theta}|=2U|\sin\theta|\). Stagnation points: q = 0 ⇒ θ = 0, π, i.e. z = ±a. Pressure: \(p=p_\infty+\frac12\rho(U^2-q^2)=\boxed{p_\infty+\frac12\rho U^2(1-4\sin^2\theta)}\). (Symmetric ⇒ zero resultant: D'Alembert's paradox.)

Q15. Liquid streams with velocity U past a fixed sphere of radius a. Find the velocity potential, the velocity on the surface, the pressure on the surface and the Stokes stream function. 10

Solution
Solution
  1. Axisymmetric Laplace solutions: \(\phi=\big(Ar+\frac{B}{r^2}\big)\cos\theta\). At infinity \(\phi\to-Ur\cos\theta\) ⇒ A = −U.
  2. Sphere fixed: \(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}=0\) at r = a ⇒ \(-U-\frac{2B}{a^3}=0\Rightarrow B=-\frac{Ua^3}{2}\).
  3. \(\boxed{\phi=-U\Big(r+\frac{a^3}{2r^2}\Big)\cos\theta}\).
  4. On r = a: \(q_r=0\), \(q_\theta=-\frac1r\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial\theta}=-\frac32U\sin\theta\) ⇒ \(\boxed{q=\frac32U\sin\theta}\); stagnation at θ = 0, π; max \(\frac32U\) at θ = π/2.
  5. Bernoulli: \(\boxed{p=p_\infty+\frac12\rho U^2\Big(1-\frac94\sin^2\theta\Big)}\).
  6. Stokes stream function (\(q_r=\frac{1}{r^2\sin\theta}\psi_\theta\), \(q_\theta=-\frac1{r\sin\theta}\psi_r\)): \(\boxed{\psi=\frac12U\sin^2\theta\Big(r^2-\frac{a^3}{r}\Big)}\). Check: \(q_r=U\cos\theta\big(1-\frac{a^3}{r^3}\big)=-\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}\) ✓, and ψ = 0 on r = a (sphere is a stream surface). ∎
